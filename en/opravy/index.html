Corrections and updates

Material substantive changes remain traceable on the article and on this page.

Corrections policy

If after publication we find a substantive error or a change that may affect the meaning or conclusion of a text, we correct it. We do not keep a public record of minor stylistic edits.

Each material correction states what changed and why. The original publication date remains separate from the update date.

Material corrections

  1. The retirement age is rising. What does that mean for a body with a different plan?
    September 5, 2026

    The exception for earlier retirement in selected category-4 risk work, including the 2,200-shift condition, was added to the detailed analysis and to the English and German versions.

  2. Three licences, three prices of error. A benchmark is not a signature.
    September 5, 2026

    The definition of legal services was aligned with Section 1(2) of the Advocacy Act, the claim about case age in the legal hallucination study was corrected, a typo was fixed and the attribution of the text was made consistent. The English and German versions were also edited.

  3. Running out of money to buy is not operational cover
    September 5, 2026

    The court sequence in the Příbram case was clarified: the District Court ruled in October 2020, while the Regional Court in Prague issued the final judgment after appeal on 3 February 2021; the official GIBS annual report was added.

  4. "Up to five years" is not the sentence imposed. A suspended sentence is not an acquittal.
    September 5, 2026

    The court sequence in the Příbram case was clarified: the District Court ruled in October 2020, while the Regional Court in Prague issued the final judgment after appeal on 3 February 2021; the official GIBS annual report was added.

  5. Czechia with Fewer Children, but Not Necessarily Fewer People: The Role of Migration
    August 28, 2026

    The headline was calibrated: demographic change means fewer children, not literally a Czechia without children.

  6. You Have Insurance. You May Not Have a Dentist. Your Postcode Shapes Access to Care
    August 28, 2026

    The Czech headline now presents postcode as one factor in access to care rather than its sole cause.

  7. One Patient. But Illness Can Change the Life of an Entire Family
    August 28, 2026

    The headline and opening now distinguish a possible family impact from a universal claim about every family.

  8. History Is Not Written Only by the Victors. It Is Also Shaped by What Survived.
    August 28, 2026

    The headline and unsupported superlatives were calibrated to distinguish the influence of victors from the selection effect of surviving sources.

  9. The City Is Not a Backdrop. Its Design Influences How We Behave.
    August 28, 2026

    The headline was changed from an absolute claim to a more precise account of how urban design influences behaviour.

  10. When the search for someone to blame outruns the search for causes
    August 28, 2026

    An unsupported quotation attribution was removed, psychological claims were calibrated, and individual accountability was separated from systemic causal analysis.

  11. What prison can do to a person — and what the evidence cannot tell us
    August 28, 2026

    Absolute claims about the loss of autonomy and the psychological effects of prison were replaced with a calibrated account of heterogeneous, mostly observational evidence.

  12. An Authentic Recording, a Wrong Conclusion
    August 27, 2026

    The model scenario was labelled in advance; the neuroscience wording, checklist count and calibration of conclusions were corrected.

  13. When postpartum psychosis disrupts the perception of reality
    August 27, 2026

    Unsupported single-cause neuroscience was removed and medical, moral and legal frameworks were separated.

  14. Numbers do not lie. But they can tell a very different story
    August 27, 2026

    The item count, the model’s time-bound conclusion and the mean-income example were corrected.
