The law targets housing need. It will not solve high housing costs on its own

The Housing Support Act targets people without adequate housing, people at risk of losing it, and some households left below the subsistence minimum after the benefit and actual costs. Not every household with expensive rent will qualify; a valid lease alone does not exclude it.

Conclusion at a glance

What is established

The Housing Support Act targets people without adequate housing, people at risk of losing it, and some households left below the subsistence minimum after the benefit and actual costs. Not every household with expensive rent will qualify; a valid lease alone does not exclude it.

What remains uncertain · What would change the conclusion

What remains uncertain

The sources alone do not isolate every cause of the observed pattern or determine how it will develop when conditions change.

What would change the conclusion

Newer comparable data, stronger causal identification or documented results of an implemented measure would change the conclusion.

The Housing Support Act can open a contact point and set an assessment of need, but that does not automatically create a vacant flat. A household with a valid tenancy may fall inside the statutory regime, or outside it, depending on its income, costs and the risk of losing its home. A high rent-to-income ratio is an important signal. It is not, by itself, a universal test of entitlement.

What we know: The Act distinguishes inadequate housing, an immediate risk of losing it, and further conditions for support measures. A high rent-to-income ratio alone is not the only statutory test.

What we do not know: Without data on the household, benefits, actual costs, legal title and the specific threat, this article cannot determine an individual entitlement. The Act also does not itself create the missing flats.

1. Need and expensive rent are not the same thing

Imagine a meeting on the Housing Support Act. The minister for regional development reads that the statute will protect 1.6 million people from falling into need and that it can help up to 53 000 households already in need. The director of the Labour Office recalls that from 1 September 2026 his office decides on support measures. The mayor of a municipality with extended powers says the contact point gives advice but has no vacant flat. A shelter worker reports that without a guarantee the owner will not sign. An analyst shows that 154 000 people in housing need is not the same as 1.6 million at risk. A tenant in a regional city, paying CZK 22 000 for a two-room flat, asks whether the law is aimed at her as well.

At first glance this is a dispute about whether the state is “finally dealing with housing”. In fact each person is describing a different object. The minister compares the instrument’s capacity with its preventive ambition. The Labour Office describes the decision agenda. The mayor describes the local stock. The outreach worker describes owners’ willingness. The analyst describes the definition. The tenant describes a market price that does not appear in a law on housing need.

The claim that “the era of affordable housing has begun” joins problems that have different instruments. Legal title, the technical fitness of the dwelling, the risk of losing housing, the share of costs in income, the availability of support and the real number of flats in the municipality have to be kept apart.

The first important distinction is therefore not whether the Act is pointless. It is what exactly is to be assessed. Otherwise we set a hostel, a market tenancy, a mortgage and a municipal flat side by side and are surprised that each wins a different contest.

Need is not expensive rent. A benefit is not a flat. Advice is not a key. And the sieve of the Act is not the market.

— Jiný Kontext

2. From legal title to an affordable flat

When the ministry website says “the law will help”, it is easy to hear that it will help everyone who pays a lot for a roof. The outcome is usually produced in a chain of layers. Each layer can raise help, lower it or change the type of error. Two people with the same rent may therefore not fall into the same column, and the same person may look different according to whether they have title, whether the subsistence minimum remains after housing costs, or whether they face a notice to quit within three months.

Five layers of one situation

  1. Physical space: whether there is a roof, water, heat, safety.
  2. Legal title: tenancy, subtenancy, hostel without protection, no title.
  3. Income after housing: what remains after rent and energy.
  4. Instrument: advice, benefit, guarantee, assistance, municipal flat.
  5. Supply: whether the municipality has a unit into which the instruments can be placed.

Space decides whether this is a matter of survival, but it is not the only criterion. A person in a shelter may meet the conditions; a household in an ordinary flat may also meet them in some circumstances, for example if after the benefit and actual costs it does not reach the subsistence minimum, or if it is at risk of losing its home. Spending 60% of income on rent does not by itself create an entitlement. A benefit can cut the cost without building a wall; a guarantee can run into missing supply.

This is where the first error in public comparisons appears. A headline announces that “the state is finally building affordability”, but in fact it is comparing a safety net with housing policy. Or it counts contact points and treats the result as a count of keys. An honest comparison must state not only the number of the Act, but also the definition of need, the capacity of the measures, the state of the stock, and what remains in the benefit system.

Separating need from the market is not decoration on a section. It decides where to turn when something fails. If the applicant did not meet § 17, the title of need may have been missing, not the willingness of the office. If they met the title and found no flat, the stock may have failed. If they found a flat and lost it within a year, assistance may have failed. The sentence “the law does not work” is the start of a diagnosis, not the end of one.

Illustrative scheme: The same rent can, in another layer, with another title and another instrument, produce another entitlement.

3. The best law is the one whose error you can see

The breadth of a political promise does not show how the system will catch a particular household. Help withheld from a person at immediate risk can have a fast and hard-to-reverse effect. For a high-cost household with a valid tenancy, the statutory conditions have to be checked first; neither the form itself nor a general cost-overburden indicator proves an entitlement. A missing flat, moreover, cannot be repaired by correcting an application.

Act No. 175/2025 Coll., on the provision of certain measures in support of housing, as amended by Act No. 129/2026 Coll., is calibrated to obtaining and keeping housing for people in need and people at risk of need. The RIA on the draft sets the aim of reducing the scale of need, preventing it, and shortening the time a household remains in it. That is the purpose of the text. It is not the purpose of regulating market rents.[1][2]

Type of failure What it looks like How to test it What limits the harm
Swapping the population 1.6 million is read as the target of direct help Separate need, risk and cost overburden Two figures in every sentence about “help”
Swapping the instrument Advice is read as a key Number of decisions vs. number of placements A trail from application to contract
Swapping the stock A guarantee is read as a new flat Number of recorded units in the municipality A map of empty flats, not a map of offices
Swapping a benefit and a wall An allowance is read as affordability Whether market rent fell, or only the top-up A separate price series and a benefit series

That also changes the evaluation question. We do not ask only which law promises the most. We ask which error in our process we will catch. Fieldwork can tolerate a narrow sieve, because a hostel cannot wait for construction. A statistician can tolerate a wide at-risk figure as prevention. A tenant paying CZK 22 000 in rent cannot tolerate both being read as her benefit.

The Act has to be judged by who meets the conditions, how long the path from application to help takes, and whether the municipality has real capacity. A clear trail from assessment to a concrete result says more than a wide headline without data on flats.

4. What the ETHOS typology measures

The FEANTSA ETHOS typology classifies homelessness and housing exclusion: roofless, houseless, insecure housing, inadequate housing. The Czech Act takes an operational sieve from this map, not the whole European vocabulary. The RIA on the Act states that in 2022 roughly 67 thousand households, with roughly 154 thousand people, lived in housing need in the Czech Republic, with an average household size of 2.28 persons. That is an estimate from sources available for 2022, not a 2026 census.[3][4]

Eurostat measures something else: housing cost overburden, the share of people in households where housing costs, after benefits, exceed 40% of disposable income. In 2024 that share was 9.2% in the Czech Republic and 8.2% in the EU. For tenants at market rent the burden is higher than for owners. This is a SILC survey, not a count of people without a roof. Adding 154 000 and 9.2% into one “housing problem” is a methodological error.[11][12]

Five facts without which a “housing crisis” is only an advertising number

  • Whether this is need under ETHOS / § 17, or a cost-to-income ratio.
  • Period and source: RIA 2022, SILC 2024, a ministry factsheet.
  • Unit: person, household, application, placement.
  • What the instrument can do: advice, guarantee, assistance, building.
  • Whether the municipality has a unit that the instrument can fit into.

For policy, therefore, a small internal count of keys matters more than someone else’s headline total of people at risk. Thirty real households from one ORP — with title, income, application date and contract date — can say more than 1.6 million on a leaflet. A public typology helps select candidates. The decision has to be made by work with the definition.

5. Compare instruments, not headlines

The table sorts housing instruments by the problem they are meant to solve: statutory need, high costs, construction or local capacity. For each it states the fact without which the result cannot be judged.

Instrument Main position What to check Typical reason for choosing it
Housing Support Act Need and the threat of loss § 17, capacity of 53 000 households To integrate the hostel and the shelter
Contact point Advice, map, confirmation Whether it ends in advice or a contract A low entry threshold
Labour Office Decision on a measure Deadline, ping-pong with the municipality Uniform decision-making
Housing benefit Share of costs in income Spending and number of recipients, not the stock To lower the bill of an existing flat
Municipal / investment stock Physical units Number of completed flats To change supply
Housing First Rapid placement + assistance Retention, not the number of briefings Stability after the key

The rows are not a verdict on the quality of the Act. The same state offers several instruments, and each can change during the year by amendment. The map is there to prevent another error: that the reader buys an excellent instrument in a product that does not see their problem, cannot finish it with a key, or locks them onto a waiting list from which it is later hard to leave.

How to read the scheme: The size of a political promise does not express the number of available flats. The diagram separates statutory assessment, a service and actual housing capacity.

6. Housing need is not expensive rent

In public debate two indicators are mixed. Housing need under the Act works with the absence of adequate housing, the threat of losing it and further conditions. Eurostat, by contrast, tracks cost overburden once housing costs exceed 40% of disposable income. That European indicator is not itself a statutory test of entitlement. Some heavily burdened households may nonetheless meet the statutory conditions on income after the benefit and actual costs, on legal title, or on an immediate risk of losing housing.[1][11]

Imagine two neighbours; this is only an illustration. Ms Marie, a single parent, lives in a market tenancy, pays CZK 19 000 and has a net income of CZK 28 000. Entitlement cannot be read from these three figures: household composition, the state social-assistance benefit, actual costs, the state of legal title and any threat of loss are missing. Mr Karel lives in a hostel without a stable legal title or income. His situation may fit the statutory categories, but even then the office must check every condition and the necessity of a measure.

The Act is aimed first at obtaining and keeping adequate housing, not at a blanket cut in market rents. That does not mean Marie can be ruled out in advance, or Karel accepted automatically. Entitlement is decided by statutory conditions, not by a stock story.

7. Anatomy of the Act: who is inside the sieve

The Act and Amendment No. 129/2026 Coll. work in § 17 with several paths: the applicant is without adequate housing and cannot obtain it; that situation threatens them in the next three months; or without a support measure they cannot obtain or keep adequate housing. Necessity of the measure and further statutory conditions are also checked. The rules around income, the benefit and actual costs mean that a valid tenancy title does not, by itself, automatically exclude a household. The amendment takes effect in its main parts on 1 September 2026; a decision therefore has to use the consolidated wording in force on that day.[1][5]

Advice at the contact point is more open than the direct measures. Landlord guarantees, contributions to deposits and housing assistance are reserved for the at-risk segment. That is not a hidden clause. It is the design. Anyone who reads a leaflet saying it “will help everyone” is reading a different document from the Collection of Laws.[2][6]

The statutory categories can include people without shelter, people in shelters and hostels, victims of domestic violence, families in inadequate premises and some households with an ordinary tenancy who meet the income and other conditions. A high rent share and the unaffordability of a mortgage do not, by themselves, create an entitlement. A specific classification cannot be decided from a single trait.

Who passes the sieve and who does not

The first test is title: does the applicant have a contract, or only an oral promise? The second is time: is loss threatened within the horizon the Act knows, or is this chronic expense? The third is what remains of income after rent. Without these three, “I need help” is not the same as an entitlement. An office that confuses them does Karel an injustice, or Marie a false hope.

8. The figures: one hundred and fifty-four thousand, 1.6 million, fifty-three thousand

Government communication works with several populations at once. 154 000 people, roughly 67 000 households, is the estimate of people in direct housing need according to the RIA from data for 2022. 1.6 million people is, in Ministry for Regional Development (MMR) materials, the number at risk of falling into need; the factsheet promises that the Act will “protect” them. The target capacity of direct measures is, in the same communication, up to 53 000 households in need. The RIA further models a fall in households in need from 67 500 towards 60 000 in 2028 and to 50 000 later; without the Act it expects a slight rise. That is a projection, not a measured result for 2026.[2][4][6]

The Act therefore does not aim a direct guarantee at 1.6 million people with expensive rent. It aims at a narrow segment and at the same time promises prevention for a wider group, mainly through advice and early capture. Those can be two true sentences. They must not be added into one.

The Czech Statistical Office (ČSÚ) records spending on the housing allowance of CZK 20.297 billion in 2024. In the first half of 2024 the Ministry of Labour and Social Affairs (MPSV) reports an average of 270.2 thousand housing allowances paid a month, at an average amount of CZK 6 122. That is a benefit system. It is not a count of guaranteed flats. Anyone who reads a falling or rising benefit bill as proof that the need Act “made housing cheaper” is measuring a different column.[13][14]

What has to be tested

The first test is the denominator: 53 000 households out of how many? Out of 67 000 in need, or out of 1.6 million at risk? The second is time: capacity “over a few years” is not the state of play in September 2026. The third is the result: the number of decisions on a measure versus the number of signed tenancies. Without the third figure, capacity is a promise, not a key.

9. A contact point is not a key. The Labour Office is not a flat

Competences shifted after Amendment No. 129/2026 Coll. From 1 September 2026 there is to be a contact point in every municipality with extended powers; a ČTK report on the Senate debate gave a rise from 115 to 205 points. Advice remains with the municipalities. The Labour Office is to keep a register of flats and decide applications for support measures. The Union of Towns and Municipalities and the Association of Local Governments warned, before implementation, of overload and of people being sent back and forth between the town hall and the office. That is an institutional risk, not proof that officials cannot give advice.[5][7][8]

Two similar counters can therefore run on different data. The municipality knows the house in the street. The Labour Office knows the file. The applicant knows both as one path. If confirmation that a flat is suitable, the decision on a guarantee and the key to the lock are split across three desks, we are measuring ping-pong even when each desk is doing its job correctly.

Before switching on praise for the network

  • Count the contact points and the average time from application to contract.
  • Separate advice from the decision, and the decision from placement.
  • Find out who records the flat and who physically has it.
  • Check that the applicant can see what stage the file is at.
  • Do not treat a map of 205 dots as a map of 205 vacant flats.

The Act’s greatest advantage may therefore not be a new power. It may be order in who is allowed to promise what. And the greatest weakness may not be the definition of need. It may be a truthful decision on a guarantee for a flat that is not in the municipality.

10. A rent guarantee does not replace a wall

A key limit of the Act is that it does not create new physical flats. Help rests on motivating private owners to let a vacant unit in exchange for a state rent guarantee and assistance. If a region has no empty flats, registers of vetted applicants without units appear. That is not a failure of the guarantee as insurance of the owner’s risk. It is a collision with supply.

Housing First and the methods of the Platform for Social Housing rest on rapid placement and support in the flat, not on waiting until the client is “ready”. This approach also needs a flat. A principle is not a stock. Czech experience with housing-first projects shows that assistance reduces a return to the hostel. It does not cut the price of a new building.[18]

The State Investment Support Fund and municipal building are another column. If the need Act is not followed by an investment programme on the scale of units the RIA assumes, the system organises a queue in scarcity. That can be said without guessing how many flats are “missing in the whole country” — such a sum would need a single definition, which public statistics do not give in one figure. It can be said that an instrument without a unit ends on a waiting list.

The OECD Affordable Housing Database shows that the Czech Republic has a low share of rental housing relative to owner-occupation and that the burden on tenants is higher than the burden on owners. That is an international comparison of structures, not an audit of Act 175/2025. It does, however, recall that a benefit and a guarantee in a country with a thin rental market hit a ceiling that only a brick can raise.[15]

11. A benefit is a raw material. It is not a housing result

The market uses several languages of help at once. The need Act speaks of a measure and a guarantee. MPSV speaks of an allowance and a supplement, later of a single state social-assistance benefit. ČSÚ speaks of CZK 20.3 billion spent on the housing allowance in 2024. The municipality speaks of flats it owns. A direct comparison that “help rose because benefit spending rose” misleads.

Cheap advice at the contact point can be excellent in a dispute over a notice to quit. The same advice is expensive if it is read as a solution to CZK 19 000 of rent. An expensive guarantee can be pointless where the owner would let the flat anyway. It can be cheaper in Karel’s case if it ends a hostel stay and cuts the cost of crisis services. The RIA estimates the social costs of need in the order of billions of crowns a year and a saving if need falls; that is a model in an impact assessment, not a line in the 2026 budget.[4][6]

The real price of accepted housing

Advice + decision + guarantee + assistance + benefit + a missing flat + waiting + a return to the hostel + future building.

The result is cheap only once the household lives under an adequate title and that can be checked. Not once a counter opens.

The costs also include churn. If the same application comes back three times for completion, the average price of a placement is not the price of one visit. If the office exhausts assistance capacity at a peak, the cost is the loss of the flat after move-in. If promises are tied hard to a headline of 1.6 million, the cost is also Marie’s loss of trust — the Act never promised her a key; she only read it that way.

It is therefore useful to measure the price per accepted key, not per number of points. Accepted means a contract, a move-in and retention. That metric can reverse a ranking that looked obvious from the factsheet.

12. How to read statistics of need

Before the Act, the reader had to trust the field. With the Act they check something more complicated: whether the figure measures need or risk, whether 53 000 is capacity or a stock, whether 205 points are advice or a flat, and whether the conclusion really follows from § 17. A figure is navigation towards evidence. It is not automatic proof.

One report can combine three true figures into a false conclusion. The first is 154 000 people in need as of 2022. The second is 1.6 million at risk in MMR communication. The third is 9.2% cost overburden in SILC 2024. Each figure works. The synthesis “the Act will solve expensive housing” is methodologically wrong. Elsewhere a report uses the number of contact points as proof that “the system is running”, even though only the front door is running.

A four-step check of a figure

  1. Existence: the figure is in the RIA, in the Collection of Laws, at ČSÚ or at Eurostat.
  2. Unit: person, household, point, decision, contract.
  3. Support: a specific passage supports a specific sentence.
  4. Scope: the year, the definition of need and what the instrument does not build all fit.

An impact check starts with a table of claims, statutory conditions and known limits. The headline should come after that. An untraceable count of households actually housed cannot be replaced by the Act’s date of effect.

Three tests of a promise

The first is definitional: does the sentence speak of need, or of rent? The second is about capacity: 53 000 households when, and with what stock? The third is contradictory: are market rents rising in the same municipality where contact points are multiplying? A product that announces effect a day earlier is not necessarily better if, a year later, no one can find how many keys came from it.

13. Supply is not a switch. It is the whole stock

The question “do we have a law?” is important but narrow. A municipality also has to know how many vacant municipal flats it has, how many private owners have entered the register, how long assistance lasts, who pays the social worker after the subsidy ends, and whether the flat returns to the same stock after a move-out. “We have a contact point” does not mean “we have a unit” or “we have retention”.

Differences between ORPs are larger than the difference between sections. Prague and an Ostrava hostel are not the same. A rural municipality with five flats in its ownership and a city with a developer who does not build rental housing need a different column. SMS ČR repeatedly points to the limits of municipal budgets. That is a local account. It is not a veto of the Act.[8]

The practical minimum is a map of the stock. It starts with the applicant, continues through the contact point, the Labour Office, the register of flats, the guarantee and assistance, and ends at the place where the household can be lost again. Without that map, a claim about support is only a description of one counter.

The Association of Rental Housing and market rent indexes measure a different series from the RIA. If commercial rents do not fall after the Act starts, that is not a falsification of the need instrument. It is information that the instrument was not aimed at the market. It would be a falsification if we had expected rents to fall because of the Act. We should not expect that in an evaluation that holds the definition.[19]

14. What to choose as an instrument

The first step is to name the kind of housing situation and the matching statutory instrument. The table proposes a comparison on the same household, in the same city and over the same time horizon.

Need First instrument Decisive test Unacceptable shortcut
Leave a hostel Support Act + guarantee + assistance A contract and retention, not a count of advice Reading 205 points as 205 flats
Prevent the loss of a flat Advice + a timely benefit Whether the notice fell or did not Waiting for need under § 17
Cut the rent share of income A benefit, or a norm Spending and what remains after housing Waiting for the need Act to cheapen the market
Raise supply Municipal and cooperative building, SFPI Completed units A guarantee as a substitute for a wall
Stability after move-in Housing First, assistance A return to the hostel within 12 months A key without a worker
A mortgage for the young Credit and tax instruments Ability to repay, not ETHOS Mixing this with need

Czech use needs a separate municipal test. A nationwide RIA will not measure whether a given ORP has a flat the Act can pay for. The test set should include a hostel, a shelter, Marie’s market tenancy, a notice within three months, and a municipality without empty units, where the right answer is “there is an entitlement, there is no flat”.

Some teams will in the end need more than one system. One for need, a second for the benefit, a third for building. That is not a failure of unity. It is the same decision as using one tool for the fire service and another for the land-use plan. The condition is that the split is intelligible, that promises do not migrate between columns, and that people know where the final key is produced.

15. A short test that says more than a press release

An evaluation can be done without a decade-long fight over a “right to a flat” and without an impulsive column that “the law failed because rents did not fall”. A short, disciplined audit is enough. Its aim is not to prove that the statute can do something impressive. Its aim is to find situations in which the promise fails, and to see whether the state can catch them.

Fourteen days from the factsheet to the waiting list

  1. Days 1–2: List 20 to 40 real households: need, risk, expensive rent, no flat.
  2. Days 3–4: Assign each a section, an instrument and whether a unit exists in the municipality.
  3. Days 5–7: Follow the trail from the contact point through the Labour Office to a contract.
  4. Days 8–10: Separate advice, the decision, the guarantee, the key and assistance after move-in.
  5. Days 11–12: Count the price of an accepted placement, including waiting.
  6. Day 13: Stress tests: a municipality without flats, a notice to quit, a victim of violence, Marie with CZK 19 000 rent.
  7. Day 14: Decide which promise may go in the headline, document the reservation and the revision date.

The evaluator should preferably not know whether the Act is to be praised. In cases of need they watch placement. In cases of risk, whether the loss of the flat did not happen. On the market, whether the market is being measured at all. Each failure is classified: a confabulated population, an omitted stock, a deadline error, or a conclusion that § 17 did not carry.

The conditions have to be comparable. The same ORP, the same month. If one factsheet is allowed to add 1.6 million and 53 000, we are testing finished communication — but we have to name it as such.

A pilot should also have exclusion conditions. For example: the single sentence “affordability has begun”, the use of SILC as proof about hostels, or an inability to count placements. A high score for a network of points must not outrank an error whose effect is a waiting list without keys.

The last output is not only a grade for the Act. It is an operating rule: what the statute is used for, what it is not used for, which household may enter the promise, what must exist in the stock, and when the evaluation will be opened again. Amendments, benefits and municipalities change. Without a revision date even a good audit slowly turns into a housing superstition.

16. Municipality, market and state: three accounts, one waiting list

A municipality has flats, or it does not. The market has listings, or excess demand. The state has a law, a benefit and a guarantee. In a headline the three accounts like to be added together. In the field they do not add. A municipality that sold its stock in the noughties cannot manufacture a wall out of a guarantee. A market that builds owner-occupied housing will not sell Karel a tenancy. A state that promises 53 000 households needs the two earlier accounts to meet, at least in part.

That does not mean the Act should wait for construction. Need cannot bear a ten-year cycle. It means the evaluation should hold two times: Karel’s crisis time and the investment time of supply. Anyone who reads the first time as the second harms Karel. Anyone who reads the second time as if the first were already done harms Marie with hope.

The government regulation on the amount of contributions under the Housing Support Act and the decree on the scope of assistance are the operating layer. They decide how much money and which activities travel with the flat. They do not change the number of flats. Anyone who does not read them does not know what happens after the signature. Anyone who reads them in place of the land-use plan is again measuring a raw material as a result.[20][21]

17. Need and unaffordability need different instruments

The first generation of housing policy sold municipal flats. The next sold a benefit. The next sold a law on need. Today’s headlines sell something larger: the impression that one statute is the whole housing market.

The contest is therefore not only over the best ETHOS definition. It also decides whether Marie stops waiting for a key the Act does not automatically promise her, whether Karel, once he meets the conditions, receives a matching support measure, and whether the municipality has real housing capacity. The more comfortable a single headline is, the higher the price of the swap can be. Waiting lists are hard to explain, and people then stop telling need from high cost.

That does not mean the Act is a bad one. A narrow sieve often creates the greatest use where a hostel and a child in a crisis facility are at stake. It means the purchase should be conscious. We need a portable definition, a documented stock, the ability to change the promise when there is no flat, and an emergency procedure without the illusion that a benefit is a wall.

The best law is therefore not the one that never leaves anyone outside. Such a text would cease to be a law on need. Nor is it the law with the largest figure on the leaflet, the densest network of points or the longest explanatory report. It is the law whose sieve matches need and whose errors of reading are visible, reversible and tolerable in the headline.

The moment the statute only advises, we are choosing a counter. The moment it sorts people, promises a guarantee, waits for a flat and teaches the newspapers what affordability is, we are also choosing a new structure of responsibility for words. And then the question is no longer: “Did the law solve housing?”

What decides is to whom the Act offers a concrete measure, where an available flat is, and what remains the task of broader housing policy.
