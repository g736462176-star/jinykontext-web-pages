The proposed 24 billion package for health insurers is not yet 24 billion of care

On 8 June 2026 the government approved a draft package meant to strengthen the public health-insurance system in 2027 by 24 billion koruna: roughly 21 billion through a higher payment for state-paid insured persons and about 3 billion by a transfer from operating funds. It is a proposal, not an act in force, and a higher receipt alone does not guarantee a shorter wait or a new practice.

The government’s total of 24 billion joins two different movements of money: about 21 billion in a proposed higher state payment and about 3 billion moved out of the insurers’ operating funds. Neither flow by itself determines the number of practices, contracted capacity or free appointments. The proposal may improve the system’s balance sheet and the availability of care may still not change at the same pace.

What we know: The proposal approved by the government on 8 June 2026 combines about 21 billion in a higher state payment and about 3 billion moved from the insurers’ operating funds. Chamber of Deputies print 235 was not, as of 31 August 2026, an approved act.

What we don't know: Until the legislative process is finished and the reimbursement rules and health-insurance plans are known, the final receipt of the individual insurers and the effect on waiting times and the regional network cannot be determined.

1. A transfer that is not yet care

Imagine a meeting about state-paid insured persons — children, pensioners and the other groups for whom the state pays the premium. The health minister points to 21 billion and says the system will break even in 2027. The finance minister points to an outlay of the state budget and says automatic indexation is being switched off for two years. The director of an insurer points to a deficit in the basic fund. A general practitioner from a district points to a full appointment book and a missing successor. A mother of two points to a list of dentists who are not registering. The journalist has the sentence “more money into healthcare” in the notebook. The journalist wants to know whether that will shorten the queue.

At first glance this is an argument about generosity. In fact each person is describing a different object. The first is talking about the assessment base. The second, about the fiscal calendar. The third, about the insurer’s liquidity. The fourth, about a post. The fifth, about travel distance. The journalist is comparing a headline with a waiting room.

A statement about “billions for healthcare” has to say whether it is a state transfer, a shift between funds, a repair of a balance sheet, or payment for a specific service. Only then can the reimbursement rules, the contracted network and staff capacity be followed.

The state budget, the insurer’s account and a free chair at the dentist are three different levels. A comparison has first to say which of them the proposal is meant to affect and by which figure the effect will be recognised.

A premium is not care. Care is not a network. A network is not a doctor. And 21 billion is not a queue in a waiting room.

2. How the proposed 24 billion package is put together

When “24 billion extra” appears on the screen, it is easy to imagine a single new payment into practices. The official government press release uses the total of 24 billion, in simplified form, for the whole package.[15] The parameters of Chamber of Deputies print 235, however, give about 21 billion in a higher payment for state-paid insured persons; another roughly 3 billion is to arise from a transfer out of the insurers’ operating funds. These are two mechanisms, not one item of the state budget.

Five layers of one transfer

  1. Assessment base and rate: the state remits 13.5% of the set base for each state-paid insured person. Print 235 proposes, from 1 January 2027, to raise the base from CZK 16 206 to CZK 18 362 and the monthly payment from CZK 2 188 to CZK 2 479, that is by CZK 291.[1][2][5]
  2. Volume of the transfer: with roughly six million state-paid insured persons the explanatory memorandum counts on about CZK 21 billion extra into the public health-insurance system. That is the system of insurers, not VZP alone.[1][5]
  3. The insurers’ balances: the money would enter the funds. The materials for the proposal model, for 2026, a system deficit of around CZK 15 billion and, for 2027, a balanced position at spending of about CZK 603.1 billion. That is an assumption in the proposal, not an actual result.[1][6]
  4. The reimbursement decree: the rules for how much is paid per point, service, centre and regulation. A transfer without a decree does not determine where the koruna will go.[10]
  5. Capacity in the field: a contract with a provider, a post, travel distance, a willingness to register new insured persons. A billion will not open this layer by itself.[3][7]

The state payment sets an important part of the receipts, but it is not the only actor. Collection of premiums from wages may grow more slowly than costly treatment. Operating funds can hold a reserve outside care. The reimbursement decree can add to a centre and not add to a rural practice. A doctor can have a contract and not have a successor.

This is where the first error of most public comparisons arises. The headline announces a contest of “strengthening care”, but in fact it compares the insurers’ receipts with the journey to a paediatrician. Or, conversely, it measures a balanced position and issues the result as a verdict on availability. An honest comparison must state not only the 21 billion, but also the year, the population of state-paid insured persons, whether the figure is a receipt or a service, and whether it was calculated by the ministry, an insurer or ÚZIS.

That is not an argument about definitions. The cut of the layers decides where to turn when disappointment comes. If the queue does not disappear, a post may have been missing. If the insurer reports a surplus, a deficit may have been patched. If 2028 freezes the payment, the manual quarrel may return. The sentence “we put 21 billion here” is the start of a diagnosis, not its end.

Illustrative diagram: The same billion can, in another fund, under another decree and in another district, produce another result for the patient.

3. The best injection is the one whose failure you will see

A government usually looks for a measure with the highest chance of an intelligible title. That is reasonable, but incomplete. Just as important is what failure looks like, whether it can be recognised and whether it is reversible. An insurer’s accounting surplus is a visible repair. A missing dentist in a district is an expensive error that the transfer may not measure at all. A freeze of the payment for 2028 can be reversed politically. You cannot relocate a board-certified doctor in one budget year.

Type of failure What it looks like How to test it What limits the harm
Swapping transfer and care “21 billion = a shorter queue” Did waiting times fall, or did only the receipt rise? Separate the fund and the network
Swapping a jump and the automatic A one-off 21 billion versus 3.2 billion from indexation Which rule applies in 2028? The calendar of the automatic
Repair instead of capacity A balanced position, the same number of posts NRPZS, ÚZIS, not only the insurer’s surplus Staff indicators
The decree filter The money sticks in another segment Where does the reimbursement actually flow? Segmentation of reimbursements
Geography A plus at headquarters, zero in the district Contracts and registrations by ORP Travel distance, not the Czech average

That also changes the evaluative question. We are not asking only whether 21 billion was missing for liquidity. We are asking which promise will fail in a way we will catch in the field. The ministry can tolerate a jump that patches a 15 billion deficit, because otherwise reimbursements would seize up. A patient cannot tolerate a balanced position becoming proof of an available dentist. Analysts can accept switching automatic indexation off as a fiscal compromise. They cannot issue it as a reform of the network.

The volume of the package is only an input. For a check it is necessary to know the calendar of the payment, the accounting path, the reimbursement decision and a measurable effect on the network. Without those steps a change in availability cannot be inferred from the headline amount.

4. What the state transfer measures

The state payment is needed because it replaces part of premium collection for people without taxable income from work. The state pays for children, pensioners, students, the unemployed and other groups named in the act — in the communication on print 235 and on the regulation for 2026 the working figure is of the order of about six million persons. That is the number of state-paid insured persons, not the number of patients in a waiting room.[2][5]

The problem begins when one transfer starts to stand for the whole of availability. The explanatory memorandum and the Chamber debate of 8 July 2026 describe the aim of restoring financial balance. The opposition, in the same reading, objected that without changes to the network the figure is not enough. Both sentences can be true. They measure a different object.[1][6]

Five facts without which 21 billion is only an advertising number

  • The exact payment per person and the assessment base, not only the sum.
  • The period: 2026 versus the 2027 proposal, the 2028 freeze, the return of the automatic in 2029.
  • The population of state-paid insured persons, not “healthcare as a whole”.
  • Whether the figure is a system receipt, an outlay of a state chapter, or a reimbursement to a provider.
  • What the number does not measure: posts, travel distance, waiting times.

For an assessment, therefore, a small breakdown of the flow matters more than someone else’s overall ranking of “we have strengthened care”. Thirty real contracts in one district can say more than one billion-item. A public transfer will help choose the debate about liquidity. The decision about care has to be made by the network.

6. Print 235: a proposed jump of 291 koruna per person

The government approved the proposal on 8 June 2026 and it was submitted to the Chamber on 23 June as print 235. In the first reading on 8 July the Chamber rejected the accelerated procedure under § 90(2). As of 31 August 2026 the proposal was not an approved act and the Health Committee, as the committee in charge, had not, according to the Chamber history, debated it; further debate was possible from 7 September. All amounts for 2027 are therefore parameters of the proposal.[1][6][15]

Key parameters from the materials on the print:

Indicator Position in 2026 Proposal from 1. 1. 2027 Change
Assessment base CZK 16 206 CZK 18 362 +CZK 2 156
Monthly state payment (13.5%) CZK 2 188 CZK 2 479 +CZK 291
Estimated extra volume — — +CZK 21 bn

The government regulation for 2026 had already raised the base from CZK 15 749 to CZK 16 206 and the payment from CZK 2 127 to CZK 2 188; the Ministry of Finance put the fiscal effect of that indexation at about CZK 4.4 billion, assuming about six million persons. Print 235 is a different movement: a legislative jump, not the ordinary automatic.[2][5]

Rough arithmetic explains the difference between the headline figures. Six million persons times CZK 291 a month times twelve months gives about CZK 21 billion in a higher state payment. The total of 24 billion arises only when about three billion moved from operating funds is added. This is a multiplication of public parameters, not an audit of the receipts of the individual insurers; the number of state-paid insured persons changes during the year.[1][2][12][15]

The materials on the print at the same time put total system spending in 2027 at about CZK 603.1 billion. In that view the year-on-year rise in spending is larger than the 21 billion of receipt from the state payment itself. It does not follow that the jump is not enough. It follows that the 2027 balance also counts on other receipts — above all on collection of premiums from wages — and on a pace of spending that the transfer does not steer. The headline “21 billion into healthcare” therefore does not measure the whole account of the year. It measures one side of one item.[1][6]

7. An indexation automatic that is being switched off

The most visible parameter is not CZK 291, but the proposed suspension of indexation. The indexation automatic introduced from 2024 raises the assessment base according to the rise in prices and half of the rise in the real wage. The materials on the print estimate that for 2027 it would have brought about CZK 3.2 billion; the proposal is to replace it for 2027 and 2028 with a one-off jump and to freeze the payment in 2028. The automatic returns from 2029 only if the amendment is adopted in this form.[1][4][5]

The proposed construction is a swap of a higher payment in 2027 for a two-year interruption of the rule. From the materials the real value of the frozen payment in 2028 cannot be determined in advance; it will depend, among other things, on the path of prices, wages and the number of insured persons.

The health minister warned in the first reading that without effect from January 2027 the insurers would not draw up insurance plans. A colleague in the government, in the same debate, spoke of the need for structural changes. Both sentences can be true. One measures liquidity. The other, the network.[6]

The 2024 automatic replaced part of the earlier manual setting. If the amendment is adopted, a two-year interruption will reduce the predictability given by the formula and further development will again depend on a legal and political decision. That is an effect of the proposal, not the present state.[4][5]

How to read the 2027–2029 calendar

According to the proposal: 2027 means about 21 billion in a higher state payment instead of the estimated 3.2 billion from the automatic; 2028 is to hold CZK 2 479 a month; from 2029 the automatic is to return. This calendar is not law in force until the amendment has passed through the legislative process.

8. Repairing a deficit is not new capacity

The materials on print 235 model a system deficit in 2026 of around CZK 15 billion and a balance in 2027 at spending of about CZK 603.1 billion. From that it can be inferred that a substantial function of the proposal is to stabilise existing liabilities; it is not possible, however, to say exactly which koruna will cover the deficit and which a new service. This is a model in the explanatory memorandum, not a measured result for 2027.[1][6]

Health-insurance plans and the insurers’ reports will only later show how the higher receipt entered the basic funds and reimbursements. It does not follow from the proposal that every koruna will create a new service, nor that the whole volume will merely patch the deficit. Both claims need subsequent data.[8][9]

System spending is also shaped by population ageing, centre-based medicines, oncology care and hospital pay. SÚKL and the reimbursement agenda for costly medicines are a different mechanism from the assessment base. If those costs grow faster than receipts, a higher state payment can stabilise the balance only temporarily; the concrete result, however, will be shown only by the insurance plans and actual accounts.[8][19]

Act No. 48/1997 Coll. sets the insured person’s entitlement and the framework of the network. The entitlement exists even when the insurer’s account is in the red — and it exists even when it is in the black and a doctor is missing in the district. The transfer changes the ability to pay the entitlement. It does not automatically change the ability to perform it.[16]

9. The reimbursement decree is a filter, not a vending machine

Even when the insurer has the money, reimbursements are shaped by the decree, contracts and regulatory rules. Without knowing their form, a distribution between centre treatment, inpatient and outpatient care or regional bonuses cannot be inferred from the transfer. The reimbursement rules can, moreover, change independently of print 235.[10]

What has to be tested after the credit

The first test: which reimbursement segment is growing year on year. The second: whether a bonus for underserved regions exists and whether it is drawn. The third: whether a contract with a new provider in the district has been added, or only the reimbursement to existing ones has risen. A product that holds a balanced position on paper can look, in the field, like a more expensive same network.

The decree is annual. The transfer from print 235 is a multi-year calendar with a freeze. A mismatch of periods is another filter: even if the 21 billion arrived in January, the reimbursement rules can change in December and again the next year. A reader who judges only the regulation on the assessment base does not see that tap. A reader who judges only the decree does not see the automatic switched off. An honest reading needs both.

10. Availability is measured by the network, not the balance

In the Czech Republic availability is often decided not by the balance on an insurer’s account, but by the geographical and contractual network. The Czech Dental Chamber has repeatedly pointed to regional differences in the registration of new patients. ÚZIS records providers and the capacities of the specialties. The OECD, in Health at a Glance, ranks spending and outcomes, not travel distance from a particular postcode. Three maps. One headline will not merge them.[3][7][13]

An illustration, not a report. In January 2027 the insurer receives CZK 291 more per state-paid insured person and the basic fund moves out of the red. In the same month a mother in a border district phones practices within tens of kilometres and hears that they are not registering new patients on the insurance. The accounting state can be a success. The factual state is the same network. The figures 291 and 21 billion are from print 235. The list of unsuccessful calls is a mechanism, not an ÚZIS census.

Statutory travel times and the insurers’ contracting policy are exactly the place where the transfer either turns into care or stays on the balance sheet. The check of this layer is not in the regulation on the assessment base.[1][10]

11. A staff ceiling cannot be credited by a transfer

Higher reimbursements can finance a service or a treatment, but they will not by themselves shorten the time of specialist training. ÚZIS and the professional organisations describe regional and age differences in capacity; this article, however, does not verify a single nationwide age average, nor prove an empty succession in every district. Staff capacity is therefore a separate indicator beside the system’s receipt.[3][11][14]

The Czech Medical Chamber, in its opinions on hospitals and primary care, speaks of shifts, board certification and limits on posts. Here too a chamber opinion is not a count of posts. It is a signal that a reader of the transfer should look for beside the explanatory memorandum.[14]

Children and older people form a substantial part of the state-paid insured, but the exact composition has to be documented by current statistics. The effect of the proposal on paediatricians, GPs or dentists has to be measured by contracts, capacities and availability by region; CZK 291 per person does not prove it.[3][22]

The time needed to prepare a doctor is longer than a legislative year. Board certification, a selection procedure, a move, the equipment of a practice. Even if the reimbursement decree offered a higher capitation in an underserved district tomorrow, the result would be on the order of years, not on the order of a press conference. 21 billion can pay that capitation. It cannot shorten board certification. Whoever promises both in one sentence promises two objects.

12. Three billion from operating funds is a different shift

Part of the proposed package is a lowering of the ceiling on the insurers’ operating funds, which is to move about CZK 3 billion into the care funds. That is a shift inside the system, not a new outlay of the state budget. Together with about 21 billion in a higher state payment it creates the government’s total of 24 billion; the actual shift and its effect will be verifiable only from the legal wording and the insurers’ accounts.[8][9][15]

The National Budget Council follows the state’s mandatory payments for insured persons as an item that returns every year. Switching automatic indexation off unhooks it from inflation and wages for two years. The return in 2029 hooks it again. The valve of the operating funds is one-off. The automatic is a rule. The rule decides the next quarrel more than three billion.[9]

13. Where to check the effect of the package

Before print 235 the reader had to check whether indexation was enough. With the print the reader checks something more complicated: whether 21 billion is a receipt or care; whether 3.2 billion is the alternative of the automatic; whether 2028 is a freeze; whether 603 billion of spending is capacity. The headline is a navigation to the flow. It is not automatic evidence about a waiting room.

Joining three documented figures can create an undocumented conclusion: +CZK 291 per person, a model-balanced year 2027 and about six million state-paid insured persons, including children, do not yet mean that children will receive more care. For that a change in reimbursements and in the network of paediatricians has to be shown.

A four-step check of a billion

  1. Existence: the figure is in the print, a regulation, or the explanatory memorandum.
  2. Object: a payment, a fund, a reimbursement, or a post.
  3. Support: year, population, unit.
  4. Scope: what is missing — 2028, the decree, the district.

14. The price of the premium is the price of the raw material, not the price of the service

The market uses several languages. The state pays a monthly amount. The insurer books funds. The provider sees a point and a regulation. The patient sees an appointment. A direct comparison of 21 billion with “strengthening care”, without a working method, misleads.

A cheap automatic (3.2 billion) can be expensive if 15 billion is missing in 2026 for reimbursements. An expensive jump (21 billion) can be cheap as liquidity and expensive as a postponement of reform. A cheap balanced position can be expensive if a freeze in the inflation year 2028 stands behind it.

The real price of care received

State payment + collection from wages + reimbursement rules + a post + travel distance + waiting time.

Care is cheap only when it can be booked. The cost includes volatility. If the same service can be booked once in five attempts, the average price of care is not the reimbursement in the decree. If the insurer exhausts a segment limit, the cost is waiting.

It is therefore useful to measure the price of the appointment obtained, not the price of a billion credited. That metric can reverse an order that looked unambiguous from print 235.

15. What to watch before the transfer becomes care

A check has first to say whether a state outlay, an insurer’s balance, a reimbursement to a provider or the availability of care is being verified. The table assigns each question its first measurable figure.

Question First object The decisive test Unacceptable shortcut
Can the insurers carry 2027? The balance, 15 vs. 21 bn Insurance plans, not the headline Saying that it is care
What happens in 2028? A freeze of the payment Inflation vs. CZK 2 479 Making 2027 the rule
Where do reimbursements flow? The decree, the segments The year-on-year change in reimbursements Assuming an even rain
Have practices been added? NRPZS, contracts Districts with children and older people The Czech average
Have waiting times shortened? ÚZIS, the insurers Specific services “More money = a shorter queue”
Is the automatic returning? The text of the act toward 2029 Whether the amendment holds the date Forgetting the switch

For Czech use a separate geography test is required. A nationwide 21 billion will not measure Jeseník or Bruntál. The test set should include the payment, the fund, the decree, the contract and the appointment — and the sentence “the source is not enough” if waiting times are missing.

16. A short test after the proposed package is introduced

An assessment can be made without arguments about character. A short procedure is enough. The aim is not to prove that the injection sounds good. The aim is to find a swap of objects.

Seven steps from the title to the sentence

  1. Write down the object: payment, fund, reimbursement, network, or appointment.
  2. Write down the year: 2026, 2027, 2028, 2029.
  3. Separate the 21 billion from the 3.2 billion of the automatic.
  4. Separate VZP from the system of seven insurers.
  5. Check the decree, not only the regulation on the base.
  6. Take one district and one specialty.
  7. Only then write whether care has been strengthened.

Three tests of availability

The first is accounting: is the fund in surplus? The second is contractual: has a contract been added? The third is about time: has the appointment shortened? A measure that passes only the first test has met liquidity. It has not met the headline about care.

An elimination error: a single sentence that turns CZK 291 into a new dentist without data on registration.

The pilot should also have a revision date. Insurance plans, the decree and the network change every year. Without a date even an honest stabilisation of 2027 gradually turns into the superstition that “21 billion went into healthcare, so care is solved”. The last output is not a winner between liquidity and the waiting room. It is a rule: which layer is paid this year, which is measured in the district, and when the automatic will be opened again.

17. The OECD measures spending. It does not measure the journey to the dentist

Health at a Glance ranks the Czech Republic by spending, risk factors and selected outcomes. It is a map of international comparison. It is not a map of travel distance from a particular municipality. A country can be solid in oncology and cardiovascular outcomes and still have holes in primary care. Both statements can be true if they are not read as one.[13]

The National Budget Council and the health-insurance plans add another axis: the state’s mandatory payment and sustainability. That too does not measure a dentist’s chair. Whoever adds the OECD, the NRR and 21 billion into the sentence “we have European healthcare, so the injection is enough” merges three definitions. Whoever turns a hole in a district into proof that the transfer is worthless merges them the other way.[8][9][13]

Eurostat measures health-care expenditure in the national accounts. Again a different definition from public health insurance in the Czech Republic, which is only part of the whole — beside household out-of-pocket payments and other sources. An international “low share of spending in GDP” is therefore not automatically praise of the efficiency of print 235. It can also be a map of what the system does not do, or of what the patient pays. An honest comparison must say which account is being added up internationally.[13][18]

18. A two-year interruption of the indexation automatic

The first generation of quarrels sold the amount of the payment for a state-paid insured person. The next generation sold the indexation automatic. Today’s amendment sells something larger: a one-off jump in exchange for a switch on the rule.

The contest is therefore not only for the 21 billion. The contest is over whether next year there will again be a manual deal. The more comfortable the headline about strengthening care becomes, the higher the price of the error. The journalist stops distinguishing the fund from the waiting room. The minister stops distinguishing 2027 from 2028. The patient stops distinguishing a promise from an appointment.

That does not mean the transfer is a bad thing. Precisely that transfer can keep reimbursements going. It means it should be read consciously. The public needs a portable legend: how much per person, which year, whether the automatic is running, where the decree flows and whether a contract has been added in the district.

The best injection is therefore not the one that never leaves a deficit. With population ageing and costly treatment we do not have that. Nor is it the injection with the highest title. It is the injection whose object matches the question and whose gaps — the freeze, the network, the staff — are visible, reversible and politically bearable.

At the moment we ask only “how much did we put in?”, we are choosing a headline. At the moment we read the flow from the budget to the appointment, we are choosing a structure of responsibility. And then the question is no longer: “Why is the 21 billion not visible in the waiting room?”

What decides is to separate the higher state payment, the internal shift of funds and the actual effect on reimbursements and the network — and not to forget that this is still a proposal.
