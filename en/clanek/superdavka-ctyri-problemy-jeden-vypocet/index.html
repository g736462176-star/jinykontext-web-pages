Conclusion at a glance

What is established

An internal analysis by the Ministry of Labour and Social Affairs, described by ČTK on 3 June 2026, reported lower calculated support for 57 % of the 155 500 households included in the recalculation. According to the same calculation, almost a fifth of the 128 400 households monitored would remain below the living minimum after housing costs. This is not a long-term record of payments actually made, nor a complete map of poverty.

What remains uncertain · What would change the conclusion

What remains uncertain

The sources alone do not isolate every cause of the observed pattern or determine how it will develop when conditions change.

What would change the conclusion

Newer comparable data, stronger causal identification or documented results of an implemented measure would change the conclusion.

One application promises less paperwork, but it does not say how much the household will have left. The form may be shorter and the calculation stricter. Digital filing may speed up the case, while the assets test decides before the components are added up. A district housing cap refines the geography and at the same time draws borders that the real rental market need not respect.

Act No. 151/2025 Coll. introduced the state social assistance benefit — in the media the unified benefit — and replaced the housing allowance, the housing supplement, the subsistence allowance and the child allowance. It took effect for new applicants on 1 October 2025. Act No. 35/2026 Coll. moved the transition of existing recipients to 1 July 2026; under the interim timetable the first payment under the new calculation was due in August for July, but this article has no operational data on whether every payment was actually delivered. From 1 October 2026 the housing-component calculation changes: instead of three bands by municipality size, 77 district caps apply.[1][2][5]

How exactly does the new algorithm combine four logics, what do the first ministerial recalculations measure, and why is a map of districts not a map of the market?

What we know: The state social assistance benefit merged four earlier benefits. An internal MPSV recalculation, reported by ČTK on 3 June 2026, described lower calculated support for 57 % of the 155 500 households in the analysed set. The publicly cited report is not a complete data table.

What we do not know: The recalculation does not show the long-term effect on all households, the number of successful remedies, or actual behaviour after the rules changed. Without the consolidated wording and specific figures, an individual entitlement cannot be calculated.

1. Four logics that look like one benefit

Imagine a meeting about the “unified benefit” at the labour ministry. The minister points to one form and says the trek between allowance, supplement, subsistence and child benefit is over. A Labour Office official points to the queue of applications and to the assets test that stops a file because of a second car. An MPSV statistician lays on the table a recalculation of transferred households and says the amount fell for most of them. A mayor from a district with expensive rents asks for a higher cap. A mayor from a district with a thin rental market asks that his median not be calculated from five contracts. The single mother is not in the room. She wants to know how much she will have left after rent.

At first glance this is a dispute about generosity. In fact each person is describing a different object. The first speaks of the number of proceedings. The second speaks of procedural capacity and the assets test. The third speaks of the year-on-year difference in a specific sample. The fourth speaks of the level of eligible rent. The fifth speaks of how robust the percentile is. The applicant measures the balance after actual housing costs.

Each person at the meeting can describe their part of the system exactly without describing the outcome for the household. The slogan “one benefit, less bureaucracy” is therefore not enough. Simplicity has to be split into the size of the components, the map of caps, the assets filter, the offset of earnings, the capacity of the office and the chance to correct an error.

Any assessment of the reform must first decide whether it is comparing the form, the calculation, the district cap, or the balance after housing. Without that choice, different quantities sit side by side.

The form is not a benefit. A benefit is not a situation. A district is not a market. And a drop in a sample is not a map of poverty.

2. How four components produce the final amount

When a single sum appears in the account, it is easy to think the “unified benefit” calculated it. The result is built in a chain. Each layer can raise help, cut it, or change the kind of error. Two households with the same income may therefore not receive the same amount. The same act can behave differently by district, by the number of cars, and by whether the household is in the vulnerable category.

Four layers of one calculation

  1. Benefit components: the act combines one to four components — housing, subsistence, child and work bonus. A household need not be entitled to all of them. One application is therefore not one entitlement.[1]
  2. Cap and percentile: eligible housing costs are capped. Until September 2026 by three municipality sizes. From 1 October 2026 by district, from data on the rents of benefit recipients; for ordinary households the 50th percentile, for vulnerable households the 75th percentile. What is measured is rent in a sample of recipients, not market listings for the whole district.[5][6]
  3. Tests and offset: income, assets, number of cars, a further residential property. The filter can stop the file before the components are added up.[1][8]
  4. Administration and channel: the Jenda electronic zone, assisted filing, deadlines, appeals, Labour Office capacity. An error in layer 4 can look like an error in layer 1.[3]

The basic formula decides an important part of the amount, but it is not the only actor. According to a ČTK report, in 2026 the president signed a change that widens the vulnerable category of single parents from care of children under seven to children under fifteen; for any concrete decision the effective consolidated wording must be checked. Such a change can alter the percentile used without changing the rent. District borders change the cap, and the number of registered cars can change whether the assets condition is met.[5]

This is where the first error in public comparisons appears. A headline announces a fight between a “more generous versus a stingier benefit”, but in fact it compares two households with a different district, a different percentile basket and a different assets filter. Or it measures only a shorter form and treats the result as a verdict on poverty. An honest comparison must state not only the name of the benefit but also the month of the calculation, the district, the size of the household, whether it is a vulnerable category, what was offset from income, and whether it was a new application or a transfer.

This map of layers is not decoration on the form. It tells you where to turn when something fails. If the amount did not cover the rent, the cap may have failed. If the application fell, the car test may have failed. If the payment arrived late, office capacity may have failed. The sentence “the unified benefit failed” is the start of a diagnosis, not its end.

Illustrative diagram: The same act can, in a different district, with a different percentile basket and a different assets filter, produce a different balance after rent.

3. The best benefit is the one whose error a household can bear

The average amount alone does not show how the system treats an error. It is necessary to know whether the household will see it, what remedy the law allows, and whether the correction will arrive before the rent is due. A week’s delay in payment can be decisive when rent is due on the first. A jump in the cap between neighbouring districts cannot be repaired by changing the form alone. Proof that a vehicle has been scrapped can correct a wrong input; loss of housing is much harder for administrative proceedings to undo.

Risk does not grow only with how often amounts are cut. It also grows when a smooth form invites undue trust that “one benefit” means “the same protection”.

Type of failure What it looks like How to test it What limits the harm
Merger error Four risks in one calculation, a non-linear response to income Compare the components separately, not only the total A mandatory breakdown of components on the decision
Sample error A percentile from a thin rental market The number of contracts in the district and size category A minimum sample, smoothing, a year-on-year cap on the jump
Filter error Rejection after a wrong figure on assets Check the legal category, ownership and the number of vehicles or properties A request to complete the file, instruction, and a remedy within the limits of the law
Channel error Digital filing the household does not complete The share of assisted filings, appeals, delays A personal channel, capacity, a temporary payment

That also changes the question of assessment. We are not only asking which benefit pays more on average. We ask which one fails in a way the household and the office can catch. An analyst may tolerate ten model deviations, because one methodological error is easy to see. A family with children cannot tolerate a single month below the living minimum after rent is paid. An office may accept a more complex formula if the decision breaks the components down. It cannot accept rejecting a file with no path to a correction.

Beside the amount of support, the readability of the decision, the chance of a lawful remedy and the effect of delay on housing must be assessed. A clear breakdown of components and instruction on the remedy may matter more to the household than a national average with no explanation.

4. What the ministerial recalculation of 57 percent shows

On 3 June 2026 ČTK published figures from an internal MPSV analysis that recalculated the entitlement of existing recipients under the new rules. This is a numerical trace before the first August payments to transferred households, not an observation of amounts actually paid. The analysis does not measure new applicants, the SILC population or the long-term path of poverty.[7]

According to the ČTK report, lower support came out for 57 % of the 155 500 households included in the recalculation. In the monitored set of 128 400 households, after actual housing costs were counted, almost a fifth would remain below the living-minimum line. ČTK obtained the figures from MPSV; the publicly cited article does not contain a complete table or a methodological file from which all results could be recalculated independently. The numbers therefore describe a ministerial model recalculation, not an audit of the August payments.[7]

Every such figure contains methodological choices. Which recipients did the analysis include? How did it set the comparison amount of the old benefits? How did it capture actual housing costs and the work bonus? Without a published table and methodology these questions cannot be closed from the ČTK article.

Five facts without which a percentage is only an advertising number

  • The exact population: households included in the recalculation, new applicants, or all households below the threshold.
  • The period: the date of the recalculation and the comparison period, not automatically the month of payment.
  • The unit: household, not person; the amount before rent, or after it.
  • The source: a ministerial recalculation, SILC, or a Labour Office audit.
  • The known limit: the full table is not public, the method is uncertain, and the rules may still change.

For an assessment of the reform, a small internal audit of the components therefore matters more than someone else’s catch-all slogan “the unified benefit failed”. Thirty real files — with actual rents, a Czech district, an assets test and the actual balance after energy — can say more than a thousand comments on the form. A public percentage helps choose the question. The decision has to be made from the file.

5. Compare logics, not forms

The old system rested on four benefits with different philosophies. The housing allowance tested the ratio of costs to income in state social support. The housing supplement covered the poorest people in material need. The subsistence allowance held a safety net. The child allowance supported families with dependent children. Act No. 117/1995 Coll. and Act No. 111/2006 Coll. kept these logics apart. Separation was confusing. It was also insurance: the failure of one benefit did not bring down the other three.[12][13]

The new act fused them into one proceeding with four components. That is an administrative gain. It is not automatically a gain for every hardship. The housing component behaves like a capped rent. The subsistence component like a minimum. The child component like conditional support. The work bonus like a correction of the earnings offset. One change in income can trigger all four at once.

Original benefit What it tested What became of it in DSSP Typical failure after the merger
Housing allowance Costs / income, municipal cap Housing component; from 1. 10. 2026 a district cap A jump in the percentile, a different data basket
Housing supplement Material need, the actual shortfall Part of the same ceiling Loss of a “last resort” above the cap
Subsistence allowance Basic needs Subsistence component The income offset pulls housing down as well
Child allowance Family, income threshold Child component A different threshold, different age bands

The rows are not nostalgia for chaos. The same state offers fewer proceedings and more mathematics. The map is there to prevent another error: a family buying an excellent form in a product that does not see its rent, cannot safely top the rent up, or locks it into a district that is later hard to leave.

How to read the diagram: The columns are not a set of accounts; they separate the number of applications from the balance after rent.

6. Four benefits, four risks

The housing allowance could fail because a municipal cap did not match the rent beyond the town. The supplement could fail by arriving late. Subsistence could fail through a hard offset. The child allowance could fail at a threshold that dropped a family after a small rise in wages. The unified benefit does not add these risks to zero. It moves them into one algorithm that responds non-linearly to a change in income.

That is why a cut in bureaucracy can be true and a worse balance can be true as well. The first measures the number of filings. The second measures the result in crowns. Act No. 151/2025 Coll. allows both, because they are different layers. Anyone who infers from the first that the second is slander confuses the channel with the benefit.[1]

The living minimum and the subsistence minimum, which the components refer to, have their own Act No. 110/2006 Coll. A change in those amounts can change the result of the benefit without changing the form. The specific amount and the date it takes effect must therefore be taken from the valid and effective wording of the regulation; neither a calculator nor a press release replaces the legal text.[14]

What needs to be tested

The first test concerns the breakdown. The same total without a breakdown of components is not the same experiment as an amount with four lines. The second concerns the rollout: an existing recipient with a deferred transfer to July 2026 is not the same as a new applicant from October 2025. The third concerns housing: rent of 16 000 crowns in a district with a high percentile basket is not the same as 16 000 in a district where the office calculates from five contracts. The act may be the same. The result is not.

7. A ministerial sample is not all poverty

The set of 155 500 households included in the internal recalculation is large, but it is not a picture of all Czech poverty. It does not include all new applicants, nor households that never apply for the benefit at all. The share of 57 % therefore describes the result of one specific ministerial analysis, not the population at risk of income poverty according to SILC.[7][15]

Almost a fifth of the 128 400 households monitored remaining below the living minimum after actual housing costs is a figure for a modelled balance, not for an average benefit. Without the full table we do not know how the results split among types of household, districts and levels of rent.[7]

In the SILC survey the Czech Statistical Office measures income and living conditions with a different definition of the household, a different period and a different poverty threshold. It cannot, without reservation, verify the June ministerial recalculation, nor refute it. These are two different statistical questions.[15]

That does not mean the ministerial number is useless. It means the sentence “for most people, help fell” needs a rider: which majority, in which month, against what total and after what costs. Without the rider it is a headline. With the rider it is a map for an audit.

8. A district is not a market. It is a statistical basket

Three bands by municipality size were crude. A municipality near Prague shared a logic with a municipality in the Bruntál area if both stayed under 70 000 inhabitants. Prague and Brno had their own category. In 2026 Lidovky published the then table of normative rent: a one-person household from 7 580 crowns in a municipality of up to 69 999 inhabitants to 12 831 crowns in Prague and Brno. The figures measure the legal ceiling, not the rent paid. From October 2026 this crudeness gives way to 77 districts, Prague as one district.[10][8]

PAQ Research pointed out that MPSV calculates the rent cap from the rents of existing recipients of the housing component, not from the wider CZSO market. For households that are not vulnerable, from the median; for vulnerable households, from the 75th percentile, plus the statutory amount for costs tied to using the flat. In a small district and a rare household size there may be few contracts. A percentile from a small sample is unstable. A year-on-year jump can arrive without any movement in listings. That is not proof that a district map is worse than three bands. It is proof that a finer map needs more robust data.[6]

An illustration, not a report: two single mothers eight kilometres apart may, at a similar rent, have a different eligible amount if they fall into different district baskets. Without the effective official values the specific difference in crowns cannot be stated honestly. Model amounts from the public debate are not official statistics, and before a practical calculation the valid regulation or the MPSV notice for the period must be opened.[6][10]

A district border is administrative. The labour market and commuting do not respect it. The algorithm does. That is the price of geographic fairness calculated from a basket, not from travel time.

9. An assets test is not a guarantee of fairness

The act limits a further property intended for housing and the number of passenger cars by the number of adult members of the household. The aim is clear: the benefit is not meant to underwrite a well-off household with a second flat and a fleet of cars. The tool is cruder than the aim. A rural family with two old cars needed for shifts and an immobile wreck waiting for ecological disposal can look the same as a speculator. The system reads the count, not the value.[1][8]

The legal text sets the assets filter, but the sources cited with this article do not, as of 31 August 2026, contain a separate public Labour Office statistic on applications rejected only because of a vehicle, nor a breakdown of the appeals that followed. From that it cannot be inferred that the filter is flawless or that it is harmful across the board; both claims would need administrative data.[1][17]

Before the filter is switched on

  • Separate the second property from the car, and the car from the wreck.
  • Check in the act how the given assets and ownership are counted for the test.
  • Document the current registration status and respond to any request from the office within the set deadline.
  • Check whether a rejection states the reason, or only “does not meet the conditions”.
  • Watch the appeals: how many of them return to payment.

An assets test can strengthen public control of how the benefit is targeted, and at the same time create borderline cases. Without data on the reasons for rejection it is not possible to say which of these effects prevails in practice.

10. A work bonus is not the same as net earnings

The act adds a bonus to the benefit for work activity and does not offset employment income in full. The aim is to remove the trap in which every crown earned disappears from the benefit. The mechanism is a correction of the offset, not a guarantee that net income after levies, deductions and rent will rise.[1][3]

Analyses of work incentives can show that the combination of levies, a fall in the benefit and other deductions creates a high effective burden for some households. They are not, by themselves, an evaluation of the effect of the Czech benefit after launch. A claim of a mass move into legal work would need follow-up administrative data and a comparable control group.[9][11]

An enforcement deduction and the calculation of the benefit are different legal mechanisms. For a particular household, the rise in disposable income may therefore be smaller than the work bonus itself. Without an individual calculation it cannot be said that the bonus will always raise the net balance, nor that a deduction will always wipe it out.

The sources cited with the article as of 31 August 2026 do not contain a public causal evaluation of how many households increased their hours because of the work bonus. Such an assessment would need a longer time series, employment data and a suitable comparison. Until then the work bonus is a described mechanism, not a proven exit from the shadow economy.

11. Where the calculation is checked after the merger

Before the merger an applicant could receive several decisions for different benefits. After the merger one decision is checked, with several components, a district basket and assets conditions. The practical question is whether the cap used, the input data and the income offset are right.

One decision can combine three true inputs into a wrong balance. The rent is real. The district is correct. The income is reported. A synthesis below the living minimum after rent may still be the result of a ceiling, not of fraud. At other times the system uses a sound median from a small sample. Or it cites a vulnerable category that will shift in October, while the file still runs under September rules.

A four-step check of the payment

  1. Existence: the decision arrived and breaks down the components.
  2. Authority: the cap is from the valid MPSV notice for the given quarter.
  3. Support: the specific rent and income actually entered the specific line.
  4. Scope: the district, household size, vulnerability, assets and month fit.

The most reliable working method therefore separates the search from the argument about the reform. First the table: old payment, new payment, rent, energy, balance, reason for the difference. Only then a verdict on the “unified benefit”. If a line cannot be opened, a claim does not become a fact merely because the form was one.

12. One application is not one situation

The Jenda client zone and assisted filing at the Labour Office are a channel. The act says the benefit can be applied for electronically or in person. The channel measures access to the proceeding. It does not measure the amount of a component. A household without a bank identity, without data and without time to appeal may have the same entitlement and a worse channel. That is an old inequality in a new interface.[3]

Older recommendations of the Public Defender of Rights on the Labour Office’s application practice point to risks around documents, deadlines, instruction and the language of decisions. They are not, however, statistics of payments or a direct evaluation of the rollout of the new benefit; for that, files and remedies have to be tracked separately.[17]

Office capacity is another layer. Act No. 35/2026 Coll. postponed the transition of existing recipients and preserved a temporary continuity of the original payments. The wording of the act itself does not prove a single political motive for the shift, nor the scale of any operational trouble in August. Delay in a proceeding would be a problem of administration, not by itself proof of a flawed formula.[2]

Two similar files may have a different channel. One household files in Jenda with a bank identity in an evening. The other waits for assisted filing, brings proof of the rent and of the car, and leaves with a case number. The act is the same. The time to payment is not. Anyone who measures the reform only by the number of clicks measures the channel of the winners of digital access. They do not measure the household for which the office is the last public counter.

13. The price of the form is not the price of the result

The market in reforms uses several languages at once. A politician pays by the number of benefits. An office pays by the number of operations. A household pays with the balance after rent. The state pays through the MPSV budget chapter. A direct comparison of “four applications versus one” without knowing the balance therefore misleads.

Cheaper administration can be economically excellent for processing a hundred thousand files. The same reform can be expensive for a household that appeals three times, uses assisted filing and in the end needs extraordinary immediate assistance. The more expensive old supplement may have been unnecessary for duplicates. It may have been cheaper for a one-off shortfall that the new ceiling will not recognise.

The real price of a payment received

Components + the balance after rent and energy + time to file + an appeal + the price of losing housing + a future jump in the percentile.

The result is cheap only when it has passed the month from which the rent is paid and can be defended with a breakdown of the components.

It is therefore useful to measure the price per accepted balance, not the price per application. Accepted means the payment met a predetermined minimum after housing, passed a check and did not cause the later loss of the flat. This metric can reverse an order that looked obvious from the form.

14. What to read in the decision before the verdict

The decision has to be read according to the kind of hardship and the specific mechanism of the calculation. The table shows in which part of the file to look for the first verifiable figure.

Need First place in the file Decisive test Unacceptable shortcut
Balance after housing Housing component versus actual rent Cap, district, percentile The average benefit in the Czech Republic
Food and basic needs Subsistence component versus the living minimum Income offset “One benefit is enough”
Children Child component, age, vulnerability Threshold and age band A single average for all families
Work Work bonus and net wage after deductions Enforcement, levies Bonus = motivation
Assets Number of cars, a further property Legal category, ownership and count Every car is abuse
Channel Jenda versus assisted filing Delay, appeal Digitisation = access

For Czech use a separate district test is needed. A national average will not measure whether the system preserves the weight of rent in the Central Bohemian Region, classifies vulnerability correctly, distinguishes a municipality near Prague from a municipality near the border, or translates “district” into a market that does not exist. The test set should include diacritics in an address, two cars, an inheritance, a small sample of contracts, and a file in which the right answer is “the source is not enough”.

Three tests of a decision after the transition

The first is comparative: what was the sum of the old benefits in the same month last year, cleaned of the change in rent? The second is residual: what is left after the actual rent and energy prepayments? The third is procedural: did the decision meet the deadline, and can the component that decided the amount be read from it? A product that pays a day earlier need not be better if, a month later, no one can find out why it paid exactly that much.

15. A short household audit

An assessment can be done without waiting for SILC 2027 and without an impulsive verdict from a single payment. A short, disciplined audit is enough. Its aim is not to prove that the reform can do something impressive. The aim is to find situations in which the algorithm fails, and to see whether the household and the office can catch them.

Fourteen days from an impression to a figure

  1. Days 1–2: List 20 to 40 real files, their district, rent, number of cars and the cost of an error.
  2. Days 3–4: Prepare the same inputs before and after October 2026, the mandatory points and an anonymous sheet.
  3. Days 5–7: Repeat each important type of household three times — with a percentile jump, with a filter, with a bonus.
  4. Days 8–10: Check Jenda, assisted filing, appeals, deadlines and whether the breakdown is intelligible.
  5. Days 11–12: Calculate the balance after rent, including energy and deductions.
  6. Day 13: Stress tests: a small sample in a district, a wreck, a child between 7 and 15 years, an incomplete entry.
  7. Day 14: Decide by the matrix, document the limits and set a date for the next review after the October caps are published.

If possible the evaluator should not know whether the case is a “winner” or a “loser” from a press conference. For the amount they watch whether the components are factually right. For housing, the support for the percentile. For the filter, whether it can be reversed. For the channel, the number of human interventions. Each failure is classified: merger, sample, filter, channel, or a missing source.

The pilot should also have conditions for exclusion. For example: a single unannounced stop of payment without instruction, use of a forbidden percentile from an empty basket, or an inability to trace from which rent the 50th percentile was calculated. A high average score for digitisation must not outweigh an error whose effect the household cannot accept.

16. Digitisation is not a benefit. It is a channel

The public-administration portal describes the benefit as a tool for covering basic needs, housing costs and the raising of children, with a bonus for work. An application is possible online or with assistance. That is a description of the entry. It is not a description of the balance. Anyone who swaps “it can be applied for on a phone” for “help has improved” has bought a channel and issued it as a benefit.[3]

The OECD compares social systems and work incentives across countries. Comparison helps only where the definitions of household, offset and housing fit. The Czech unified benefit, with a district percentile basket from recipients’ rents, is not the same as a Canadian or Danish model with a different market. When the definitions fit badly, it is more honest to say so.[18]

One application saves time for programmers and for some officials. If the formula misses a specific hardship, a system appears that is smooth in the channel and blind on the ground. The quality of a net is not known by how easily a form is filled in. It is known by whether it catches a person in the month when a notice to quit the tenancy is a threat.

17. The district cap as a long-term risk

The first generation of social policy sold the number of benefits. The next sold targeting. Today’s reform sells something larger: a place in which four hardships are added up in one algorithm and geography becomes a parameter of the calculation.

So the contest is not only over the amount of help. It is over the data basket from which the percentile is calculated, the definition of vulnerability, the number of cars and the habit of applying once. The more convenient the form that appears, the higher the price of leaving the map of 77 districts. Files, automatic rules and expectations are hard to return to four acts. People stop distinguishing their own hardship from the product in which the state calculated it.

That does not mean the merger is wrong. It is precisely the merger that can cut friction. It means it should be bought with open eyes. The state needs a portable breakdown of components, a documented percentile, a way to repair the filter and an emergency procedure when the basket in a district runs dry.

The best benefit is therefore not the one that never short-changes anyone. We do not have such a system with four logics merged. Nor is it the benefit with the shortest form, the highest average or the largest number of districts. It is the benefit whose components match the real hardship and whose errors are visible, reversible and bearable in the month the rent is due.

The moment the state is only merging forms, it is choosing a channel. The moment it reads rent from a basket of recipients, filters cars and prepares the October percentiles, it is also choosing a new map of fairness. And then the question is no longer: “Is one benefit simpler?”

In a check, what matters is which hardship the formula misses, how the district entered the result, and whether the error can be corrected before the rent is due.
