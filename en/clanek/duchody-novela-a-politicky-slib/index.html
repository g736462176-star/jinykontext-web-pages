What the government’s first pension amendment of July 2026 actually contained

In its policy statement the government coalition pledged to cap the retirement age at 65 and to return to indexation by half of real-wage growth. The public summary of the first amendment approved by the government on 20 July 2026 describes an earlier spread of the age supplement from age 80 and an increase in the percentage assessment for work in retirement. The cap at 65 is not in it, and according to a ČTK report the return to half of wages dropped out of the bill.

A pension reform and a partial amendment are not synonyms. A headline may announce government approval while the bill does not contain a cap at 65. An age supplement may be part of the first phase, indexation by half of real-wage growth may drop out of it, and the increase for work may have its first practical effect only in 2028.

Andrej Babiš’s government approved a policy statement on 5 January 2026: the retirement age will be capped at 65 and the indexation formula with inflation and half of real-wage growth will be restored. On 20 July 2026 the government approved an amendment to Act No. 155/1995 Coll. on pension insurance. Public summaries by ČTK and the labour ministry describe an age increase from 80 and automatic raising of the percentage assessment for work from 2028. ČTK reported that the return to half of wages dropped out of the bill after comments from the Ministry of Finance; MPSV places the cap at 65 in the second phase.[1][3][4][18]

How does a reform become a shift in the timing of a supplement, what do 2.6 billion koruna in 2027 measure, and why is postponement not the same as fulfilment?

What we know: The policy statement promises a cap at 65 and half of real-wage growth. The official MPSV summary of 20 July 2026 describes, in the first amendment, age supplements, work in retirement and payments abroad; a separate MPSV page places the cap at 65 in the second phase. ČTK described the dropping of half of wages. On 3 August 2026 the Office of the Government still spoke of age bonuses and the increase for work as a proposal.[1][3][4][18][19]

What we do not know: This article does not have as its basis a definitive Chamber wording with a print number and all sections. It therefore assesses the publicly documented content of the bill as of 20 July 2026, not the final form after parliamentary changes. Budget figures are scenarios quoted by ČTK, not certain future spending.

1. A pledge the amendment need not carry

Imagine a July meeting on the “pension amendment”. The labour minister puts the supplement from age 80 on the table and says that seniors at a high age will get the money sooner. The finance minister points to a long-term projection and says that the cap at 65 and half of wages do not belong in this text. The prime minister promises a second wave and a mention of Brussels. A coalition deputy hears that the government is dealing with pensions. An opposition deputy hears that the January pledge has vanished. The journalist in the room has two sentences in the notebook: “The government approved a pension amendment” and “we will cap the age at 65”. He wants to know which one holds.

At first glance this is a dispute about the word reform. In fact each person describes a different object. The first speaks about an age increase in the percentage assessment. The second speaks about the balance of the pension account in the 50s of this century. The third speaks about the legislative calendar. The fourth speaks about political delivery. The fifth speaks about a missing section. The journalist compares the headline with the policy statement.

Participants in the debate may therefore speak truthfully about different parts of the bill and still leave the public without an answer as to which programme points actually became sections. The term “pension reform” here covers several separate topics: retirement age, the indexation formula, arduous occupations, work in retirement and the age supplement.

Scrutiny therefore begins with the question of which pledge entered the bill and later the law in force. A programme, a government bill, an explanatory memorandum and a valid act have different legal weight.

A programme is not a law. A headline is not a section. A supplement is not an age cap. And postponement to 2028 is not fulfilment in 2026.

2. From programme to effect several steps remain

When “pension amendment” appears in a headline, it is easy to get the impression that the system has changed. The result, however, is produced in a chain. Each layer can fulfil, postpone or merely rename a pledge. Two documents on the same topic therefore need not say the same thing.

Five layers of one pension verdict

  1. Policy statement: a political document of 5 January 2026. It promises a cap at 65, half of real-wage growth, age indexation from 80, arduous occupations and support for work in retirement. It is not a statute. It does not change the assessment.[1]
  2. Government bill amending Act No. 155/1995 Coll.: a bill approved by the government on 20 July 2026. In its public summary MPSV lists the age supplement, work in retirement and payments abroad; it places the cap at 65 in the second phase. ČTK reported that half of wages dropped out of the bill after finance comments.[3][4][18]
  3. Effect: the age increase from 1 January 2027. Automatic raising for work first in January 2028, the relevant period of work from September 2026. A calendar is not a headline.[5][6]
  4. Fiscal layer: the explanatory memorandum and projections. ČTK, on government approval, stated that annual spending on both measures contained would, on the mildest scenario, grow from 2027 to 2060 from 2.6 billion koruna to 60.8 billion. This is a scenario for this amendment, not the price of the cap at 65.[3]
  5. Administration and responsibility: ČSSZ, automatic recalculation for employees, an application for the self-employed, a second wave promised for later. Who bears the bill depends on the date, not on the name of the act.

The basic pledge determines an important part of the debate, but it is not the only input. The government can approve a bill without a cap at 65, keep the 6.5 % insurance discount and at the same time propose an increase in the percentage assessment for further work. The label “first wave” itself shows that this is not the whole programme. A comparison must therefore state the date, the stage of the legislative process, the content of the bill, the intended effect and the points expressly postponed.

The individual steps make it possible to determine exactly what has changed. A supplement from age 80 documents an age change, but by itself says nothing about the retirement age. The indexation formula in force cannot be inferred from the programme. The sentence “the government approved a pension amendment” therefore does not yet describe the content or the effect of all pledged points.

Illustrative diagram: The same word reform can, in a different layer, with a different effective date and a different missing section, produce a different impact.

3. Check also the points that are missing from the bill

A visible supplement is easier to check than a provision that is missing from the bill. A five-hundred-koruna amount at eighty has a concrete sum and a date. A missing cap at 65 appears only when the programme, the bill and the retirement age in force are compared. A postponed point remains exposed to further political change.

The risk does not grow only with the absence of a pledge. It also grows when a grand title invites undue confidence that the headline has replaced the section.

Type of failure What it looks like How to test it What limits the harm
Title error “Reform” for a parametric shift Compare programme and section line by line A table of pledge / text / effect
Cap error A supplement from 80 is read as a cap at 65 Open the retirement age in 155/1995 Coll. One sentence: what the amendment does not change
Calendar error Effect in 2027 is read as indexation in 2027 The date as of which January indexation is set Separate the age supplement and the wage formula
Account error 2.6 bn is read as the price of the whole programme The scenario for both measures contained versus the dropped items Two columns: in the text / outside the text

This also changes the reader’s question. We do not ask only whether the amendment “gives something”. We ask which gap in our reading we will catch. An eighty-year-old woman may tolerate that the cap at 65 is not her question. A forty-year-old man cannot tolerate that the headline answered this question for him. A state accountant may accept 2.6 billion in 2027. He cannot accept them as proof that half of wages is settled.

Public assessment must, besides the changes contained, also show the missing points, their expected date and the impact on different cohorts. An accurate description of a more modest bill is more useful than a broad title that conceals its limits.

4. What a headline does not say about a section

A news headline summarises a government decision, not the entire pension system. On 20 July 2026 ČTK described approval of a bill raising pensions after eighty and for work in retirement, and the dropping of a return to higher indexation.[3] From this report it does not follow either that all programme points have been fulfilled, or that the bill brings no change at all.

Every headline contains hidden decisions. Does it cite the programme, or the bill? Does it speak about 80 years, or about 65? About 2027, or 2028? About ČSSZ, or about Brussels? The answer to any of these questions can change the order of blame and credit.

Five facts without which “reform” is only an advertising word

  • The exact document: programme, government bill, Chamber print, or the act in force.
  • The date: 5 January, 20 July, 1 January 2027, 1 January 2028.
  • The population: seniors 80+, working pensioners, cohorts heading towards 67, arduous occupations.
  • What the text changes and what it expressly does not change.
  • The fiscal scenario: to which measures the 2.6 billion relate.

For buying political calm, therefore, a small internal table matters more than a foreign overall slogan. Five real rows — with actual sections, Czech effective dates and a real gap — can say more than a thousand debates about “betrayal of pensioners”. A public headline helps select candidates for a sentence. The decision must be made by work with the text.

That is not proof that press conferences have no value. It is an audit of one specific government day carried out by public agencies, not proof that pledges have no value. It does show, however, that even the precise date of 20 July may precisely change only part of the question the programme opened in January.

5. Compare sections, not pledges

The following comparison does not assess who is generally fairer to seniors. It shows what type of information individual documents contain and what, conversely, cannot be inferred from them.

Document Main working position What to verify Typical reason for the choice
Programme 5. 1. 2026 Political commitment Cap 65, 1/2 of wages, occupations One intelligible sentence for voters
Government amendment 20. 7. 2026 Sections of 155/1995 Coll. What is in the text and what is missing What the Chamber actually receives
Act 155/1995 Coll. in force Retirement age, indexation 1/3 The trajectory towards 67 The state that holds until it is changed
Explanatory memorandum / ČTK Fiscal scenarios 2.6 bn for what; 2–102 bn for what The price of what is contained, not the price of the pledge
Print 50 (10th term) A different bill, different sponsors It is not the government wave of July Do not confuse an opposition print with a government one

The rows are not a verdict on honesty. The same government offers several documents in one year and each of them may change during reading in the Chamber. The map is meant to prevent a different error: that a voter buys an excellent headline in a product that does not see his retirement age, cannot change it safely, or locks it into a “second wave” from which it is later hard to exit.

Interpretive diagram: The position of the documents does not express a score; it shows questions that must be answered together.

6. A policy statement is not a statute

The government’s text of 5 January 2026, in the chapter on pensions, promises capping the retirement age at 65 and restoring an indexation formula that takes account of living costs and half of real-wage growth. It further promises age indexation from 80, an adjustment of arduous occupations and an increase in the pension for work. It is a document that the government sends to the Chamber with a request for confidence. It is not an amendment. It has no effect. It does not change the ČSSZ assessment.[1][2]

That does not diminish its importance. It diminishes the warrant for claiming that approval of any pension amendment is automatically fulfilment of the January sentences. A programme may be fulfilled in several steps. It may be fulfilled in part. It may be postponed with a reference to fiscal rules and the European Commission. Each of these options is politically defensible. None of them turns a headline into a section.

In May 2026 Lidovky described that the MPSV bill then contained only some points of the programme and that the cap, arduous occupations and pillars were to come later, with effect from 1 January 2028. Between May and 20 July, half of wages also disappeared from the bill. The trail is therefore double: something never entered the first wave at all, and something dropped out of it during comments. To confuse both for a single “betrayal” is inaccurate. To separate both is the reader’s work.[7][3]

7. The age supplement: earlier payment of the same cap

The most visible change in the July bill is an adjustment of the monthly pension amount after a set age is reached. The existing rule raises the monthly pension by 1 000 koruna on reaching 85 and by a further 2 000 koruna on reaching 100. From 1 January 2027 the bill spreads the same resulting monthly level of increase: by 500 koruna on reaching 80, 85, 90 and 95 and by 1 000 koruna on reaching 100. These are not one-off amounts paid out, nor a “lifetime sum” of 3 000 koruna; each increase feeds into the next monthly pension payment.[3][5][18]

ČTK reported an estimate that the addition by age would in 2027 cost nominally 2.6 billion koruna. The sum would grow gradually: in 2029 three billion, in 2035 then 3.8 billion, by mid-century 4.8 billion, in 2060 then 6.4 billion. This is a projection for this measure, in the then prices of the scenario, not a ČSSZ audit. It does not measure the average pension. It does not measure whether 500 koruna at 80 is “dignity”. It measures the budgetary flow of the age step.[3]

The mechanism mainly changes the timing and the path of the monthly increase. A person who reaches one hundred is, under the bill, to have against the basic pension the same resulting monthly level of increase of 3 000 koruna as under the existing age steps, but receives part of the increase earlier. It is not a lifetime maximum of money paid out. Anyone who adds the amounts into the sentence “pensions will rise by three thousand extra” confuses the monthly level after the age is reached with a one-off or lifetime amount.

How to read the age supplement

Separate three numbers. The first is the monthly step: 500 or 1 000 koruna after a specific age is reached. The second is the resulting monthly level of increase of 3 000 koruna at 100. The third is the estimated annual budgetary flow of 2.6 billion koruna in 2027, which ČTK relates to the age measure, not to the dropped half of wages. Anyone who adds them into one sentence about “reform” produces an amount that none of these figures means.

An illustration, not a report: Mrs Marie is 79. A headline about supplements for seniors is concrete for her. At 80 she may receive 500 koruna a month. Mr Tomáš is 42. He reads the same headline and may gain the impression that the retirement age has stopped. For him nothing about 65 changes in this amendment. One title, two populations, two objects.

8. Work in retirement is not an insurance discount

Since 2025 a 6.5 % discount on social insurance has applied to working old-age pensioners. From the first automatic recalculation in January 2028 the July bill is to raise the percentage assessment of the pension by 1.5 % for every 365 days of gainful activity; we do not use public government and ministry summaries here to derive a more precise calculation base, which the section wording must confirm. The discount is to remain. For employees ČSSZ is to carry out the increase automatically; the self-employed are, according to the summary, to proceed by an application tied to the statement of income and expenditure.[3][18][19]

The relevant period of work is to be counted already from September 2026, or from a later month according to promulgation of the act. The first automatic raising of pensions is to come in January 2028. Minister Juchelka argued that it had been wrongly assumed that people would set aside the money from the discount. Raising the assessment is to ensure more money in old age. That is a hypothesis about behaviour, not a measurement of household savings.[3][5]

The catch is in the calendar. Postponement to 2028 means that real fulfilment is borne only by the budget of a later period. That may be legislatively rational: the rules of January indexation and recalculation have their deadlines. It is also a political shift of the bill. Anyone who announces “we will reward work in retirement” in 2026 has announced an intention. He has not announced that the assessment rose in 2026.

Two similar accounts may therefore have a different regime. An employee with a monthly ČSSZ report gets the automatic. A self-employed person with a statement of income must apply. The 6.5 % discount flows into net pay now. The 1.5 percent flows into the assessment later. Anyone who adds them into an “immediate reward for work” mixes the wage flow with the pension flow. The law may hold both. A headline must not present both as the same moment.

The previous regime until the end of 2024 raised the pension for work in a different way. The proposed 1.5 % for 365 days from the first recalculation in 2028 is a new parameter beside the retained discount. The old regime, the discount effective from 2025 and the proposed increase must therefore not be added into one sentence about a “return of the old times”.

9. What dropped out is not a trifle in an annex

A return to half of real-wage growth instead of a third was explicit in the policy statement. In the comment procedure the Ministry of Finance wanted more precise calculations and opposed inclusion in this amendment. ČTK quoted that annual spending would, according to the labour ministry, rise from 2029 to 2060 from two billion koruna to 102 billion. This is a long projection for the dropped measure, not the deficit of 2027. Government politicians said that the step, like the cap at 65, would come in a further amendment.[3][4]

A cap at 65 would abolish the mechanism in force of gradually raising the age towards 67 for younger cohorts, which a previous amendment brought into the act. As late as 11 February 2026 Minister Juchelka in the Chamber still placed the cap in the first wave. In March he was already speaking of a second wave with arduous occupations. In June on Czech Television he said there was no need to hurry. On 20 July the government approved the first amendment without the cap. The trail is public. It is not proof of bad intent. It is proof of a change of calendar.[4][8]

Arduous occupations are also not in the July text. The programme promised them. In May MPSV placed them in a later wave. Anyone who reads “pension amendment” as protection for a welder or a nurse is reading a document that is not in this wave. The gap is empirical: it is missing from the text. An assessment of intent is political. Separating both is again the reader’s work.[7][1]

10. Demography is not a press conference

In the Report on the Long-Term Sustainability of Public Finances 2025 the National Budget Council described that the “small” and “large” pension reforms of previous years — tightening of early pensions, reducing the share of real wages from a half to a third, gradual raising of the age towards 67 and adjustment of the replacement ratio — improve the balance of the pension system in the worst years, at the turn of the 50s and 60s of this century, by roughly two percentage points of GDP. Without these measures, according to the council, the deficit of the pension system in that period would reach almost 4 % of GDP. With the sum of the reforms the projection heads towards the 1.5 % of GDP boundary. This is an NRR projection, not the treasury of 2026. It does not measure fairness to today’s eighty-year-olds. It measures the path of the account.[9]

Abolishing the path towards 67 and returning to a cap at 65 would shift the long-term account towards a more costly variant. In publicly available materials located as of 30 August 2026 we did not find a separate methodologically described dataset from which the precise billions “extra in 2050” quoted from an individual government slide could be verified. We therefore keep to the NRR projection: it works with percentages of GDP in the period when the departure of large cohorts culminates, not with the account for a 500 koruna supplement. Mixing the 2.6 billion of 2027 with this long-term path would join two different calculations.

CZSO population projections show ageing and the dependency ratio. ČSSZ pension statistics show counts and average assessments as of 31 December. Neither of these sources measures whether 65 is “fair”. They measure population and payments. Policy may read various conclusions from them. The data read from them only what they measure.[10][11]

The European Commission in the Ageing Report sets Czech paths beside other member states. The comparison holds only where the definitions of statutory age, early departure and the replacement ratio fit. When they do not fit, the ranking misleads.[12]

11. A postponed pledge is checked in another phase

Before the July approval a voter had to watch whether the government would “do something about pensions”. After approval he watches something more complicated: whether the headline fits the section, whether the effect fits the year, and whether the second wave has a date, or only an adjective. The pledge was not cancelled by a press conference. It moved into the gap between documents.

One report can combine three true sentences into a false conclusion. The government approved the amendment. Eighty-year-olds will get 500 koruna. The programme promised 65. Each sentence works. The synthesis “they fulfilled the pension reform” is methodologically wrong. At other times 2.6 billion is quoted as the price of the “whole reform”, even though the scenario belongs to both measures contained, not to the dropped half of wages. Or the 6.5 % discount, which already applies, is quoted as if the July text had brought it.

A four-step check of a pension statement

  1. Existence: the sentence is in the programme, in the bill, or only in the headline.
  2. Authority: primary is the government text and the act, not a paraphrase.
  3. Support: a concrete passage supports a concrete pledge.
  4. Scope: the population, the effective date and the fiscal scenario fit.

The most reliable procedure separates searching from writing. First a table of pledges and gaps. Only then a dispute about betrayal. If a section cannot be opened, a claim is not promoted to fulfilment merely because the government “sat”.

12. Postponement is not repeal. It is a different account

A second wave may come. It may contain the cap at 65, arduous occupations and half of wages. It may contain only a part. It may not come. The state as of 20 July 2026 is: a first wave without these points, a public promise of later, a mention of alignment with fiscal rules and with the Commission. Postponement is a political technology. It is not an accounting zero. It moves the conflict into another year, to another majority and to another notification cycle.[4][8]

Before the second wave

Write down which three points of the programme are missing from the first wave: the cap at 65, half of wages, arduous occupations. For each, demand an effective date, a fiscal scenario and whether it is a pledge or a section. Until a date is missing, it is honest to say “postponed”, not “settled”. A second wave without a calendar is a headline. It is not a statute.

In 2024 the Constitutional Court assessed the previous pension amendment No. 270/2023 Coll. That is a different print, a different Chamber, a different dispute about indexation. The number 270 in the Collection from 2023 must not become the automatic address of the July bill of 2026. As of 25 July 2026 the Chamber system, for the pension insurance act in the 10th term, recorded primarily deputies’ print 50 — a bill by a group of deputies from December 2025 on shortening the insurance period and indexing the child-raising increment, to which the government gave a negative opinion. The government July amendment may have received a number in the register later; as of the date of writing this text it is not honest to present the old 270/2023 or print 50 as “that” government wave. These are three objects.[13][14]

Postponing the indexation formula to a practical effect in 2028 — even if half of wages returned to the text — shows the same calendar mechanics. The rules of January indexation are set with a lag. Effect from 1 January 2027 therefore need not catch indexation in 2027. That is a legislative detail that changes the year of the account. It is not a trifle in a footnote.[15]

13. The price of a parameter is not the price of a pledge

The market uses several price languages at once. A senior pays with a visible five-hundred-koruna amount. The labour minister pays with a scenario of 2.6 to 60.8 billion for both measures contained. The finance minister pays with the absence of 2 to 102 billion for half of wages. The demographer pays with percentages of GDP in the 50s of this century. A direct comparison of these price lists without the layer misleads.[3][9]

A cheap supplement of 500 koruna may be economically excellent as targeted support for a high age. The same amendment may be expensive as a substitute for an unfulfilled age cap, if it is read that way. An expensive return to half of wages may be unnecessary in a year of high inflation. It may be cheaper as fulfilment of a pledge, if it reduces the number of further amendments and disputes about confidence.

The real price of the adopted text

Measures contained + measures postponed + the calendar of the account + the price of the demographic path + the price of mistaking a headline for a law.

The result is cheap only when it passes a check of gaps and can be defended with the same content in the programme and in the section.

It is therefore useful to measure the price of an accepted pledge, not the price of a headline. Accepted means that the measure is in the text, has a date and does not serve as a screen for an item that is not in the text.

14. What to read in the bill before a verdict falls

Reasonable reading does not begin with the word reform, but with the kind of pledge and the control mechanism.

Need First place in the text Decisive test Unacceptable shortcut
Fulfilment of the programme A table of pledge / section Cap 65 and 1/2 of wages “They approved an amendment”
Income of eighty-year-olds Age steps 500 / 1 000 Kč Cap 3 000 Kč, date 1. 1. 2027 “Three thousand extra as of today”
Work in retirement 1.5 % and the 6.5 % discount Effect 2028, self-employed versus employees “Already this year”
Retirement age 155/1995 Coll. in force The trajectory towards 67 A headline about seniors
The long account NRR, Ageing Report % of GDP in the 50s of this century, not 2.6 bn One number for everything
Arduous occupations Whether they are in the text at all Does the second wave have a date? Silence = fulfilment

For Czech use a separate generation test is necessary. An English Ageing Report will not measure whether a headline in Czech promises 65 to an eighty-year-old woman. The test set should contain the January programme, ČTK of 20 July, the retirement age in force, 2.6 billion with the reservation of a scenario, and a file in which the correct answer is “it is not in this wave”.

What needs to be tested

The first test concerns the title. The same word reform in the programme and in the amendment is not an identical experiment. The second concerns the calendar: 2027 for the supplement is not 2028 for work. The third concerns dropped items: whether they have a date of a second wave, or only a promise. A product that adds to a senior a month earlier need not be fulfilment of a pledge that concerned the retirement age.

15. Fourteen days from headline to section

A position on the amendment can be assembled without waiting for 2050 and without impulsive agreement with a briefing. A short reading pilot is enough. The aim is not to prove that the government can add to seniors. The aim is to find the gaps in which the headline fails.

  1. Days 1–2: List the pledges of the programme and the rows of the July bill.
  2. Days 3–4: Separate the populations: 80+, working pensioners, cohorts towards 67, arduous occupations.
  3. Days 5–7: Mark each pledge: in the text / dropped / never entered / postponed.
  4. Days 8–10: Verify effect, the 2.6 bn scenario, the 2–102 bn scenario and what they belong to.
  5. Days 11–12: Open the retirement age in force and the NRR on 1.5 % versus 4 % of GDP.
  6. Day 13: Stress: confusion of print 50, confusion of 270/2023 Coll., confusion of the 6.5 % discount for a novelty.
  7. Day 14: Write down the reading rule and the date of the next review — after the Chamber, not after X.

The pilot should have elimination conditions. For example: a single sentence that turns a 500 koruna supplement into a cap at 65, or the use of 2.6 billion as the price of the dropped indexation. A high average score of “the government approved something” must not override a gap whose impact the reader cannot accept.

16. Two prints, two amendments, two questions

The Chamber of the 10th term sees in parallel deputies’ print 50 on pension insurance and the government wave of 20 July. The first shortens the insurance period and reaches for the child-raising increment. The second walks in age steps and work in retirement. The government expressed a negative opinion on print 50. Anyone who adds them into a “pension reform 2026” produces an object that is not in the register.[14]

Act No. 270/2023 Coll. is an amendment from the previous term on indexation, later assessed by the Constitutional Court. The number can mislead, because media shortcuts and the memory of public debate easily join distinct documents. The July bill of 2026 must therefore be labelled as a government bill until the Chamber publishes its print number, and the number 270 left to the amendment of 2023.[13]

OECD pension surveys show that parametric adjustments — age, indexation, work in retirement — are common and that they are often delivered in parts. Partiality is therefore not a Czech aberration. A Czech aberration would be to present a part as the whole of the programme of 5 January.[16]

Analyses by IDEA at CERGE-EI describe that a shift of age and a change of indexation land differently by cohort, sex and the amount of the pension.[17] They do not thereby automatically assess the July bill. They do, however, recall the difference of target groups: a supplement from 80 aims at today’s high age, a cap at 65 at future retirement, and the indexation formula at the whole set of pensions paid. A bill containing only the first point may be a substantive change, but it does not thereby fulfil the whole programme.

17. Postponement changes political responsibility

In pension policy it is not only the parameter that decides, but also the order of legislative steps. A pledge moved to a later phase may be deliberated in a different political situation than a bill approved today.

The contest is therefore not only for the highest supplement. The contest is over which pledge enters the first wave, which the second, and which remains in the programme as an anchor. The more comfortable the headline that arises, the higher the price of leaving the impression that 65 is done. Cohorts, ČSSZ automatics and expectations are hard to reverse. People stop distinguishing their own entitlement from the product in which the government announced it.

An age supplement may bring an earlier increase to people at a high age. Its benefit must not, however, conceal that the retirement-age cap and the indexation formula remained outside the first phase. The public therefore needs an overview of points contained and postponed, their legislative status and dates.

A bill cannot be assessed by the breadth of its title or the number of pledges in a press sentence. What can be checked is whether its sections correspond to a specific measure and whether the missing points are visible, dated and politically acknowledged.

At the moment when the government is merely adding 500 koruna at eighty, it is choosing a parameter. At the moment when it postpones the age cap, drops half of wages and transfers the account for work to 2028, it is also choosing a new structure of responsibility among generations. And then the question is no longer: “Did the government approve a pension amendment?”

Let us ask: Which pledge already has a section and an effective date, which remains in a further phase, and which so far exists only in a political message?

— Jiný Kontext
