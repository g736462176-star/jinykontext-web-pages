The proposed deficit of CZK 389 billion sits below the psychological line of four hundred billion, but CZK 79 billion above the approved plan for 2026. The cash deficit, the general-government balance and the structural balance are different indicators. Meeting one European threshold does not by itself prove that nominal debt, or the cost of servicing it, is falling.

At 11:00 on 31 August 2026 the Ministry of Finance published the draft state budget for 2027. It reports revenue of CZK 2 189.1738 billion, expenditure of CZK 2 578.1738 billion and a deficit of CZK 389 billion. Act No. 38/2026 Coll. approved a deficit of CZK 310 billion for 2026, so the proposal assumes a year-on-year increase of CZK 79 billion. The ministry has submitted the document to the government; the figure may change in government and parliamentary debate.

How is a budget narrative produced, what do four different deficits measure, and what happens to the spending framework when defence becomes an exception?

What we know: The Ministry of Finance proposal of 31 August 2026 works with a deficit of CZK 389 billion, revenue of CZK 2 189.1738 billion and expenditure of CZK 2 578.1738 billion. Against the approved deficit for 2026, the proposal is CZK 79 billion higher.

What we don't know: We do not know the final shape after government and parliamentary debate, nor the actual outturn for 2027. The ministry’s claims about growth, savings or the effect of spending are assumptions in the proposal, not a measured result.

1. A ceiling under which the direction can still deepen

Imagine the meeting after the 2027 budget proposal is published. The finance minister puts a deficit of CZK 389 billion on the table. The chair of the National Budget Council points to the fiscal-responsibility rules. The defence minister recalls the statutory share of two percent of GDP and the European defence clause. The coalition talks about investment, the opposition about stalled consolidation. The journalist has, side by side, the crisis year 2021, the approved plan for 2026 and the proposal for 2027. What can actually be compared?

At first glance this is an argument about discipline. In fact each person is describing a different object. The first is talking about a psychological threshold below which the deficit still “fits”. The second, about the structural balance and the spending framework. The third, about the defence chapter and NATO methodology. The fourth, about visible spending. The fifth, about the year-on-year direction. The journalist is comparing one headline with another.

The same proposal can meet one threshold and worsen another indicator. The phrase “responsible deficit” therefore needs a legend: the cash balance of the state budget, the general-government balance, the structural balance, the statutory spending framework and the cost of servicing the debt.

So the first distinction that matters is not who is cheating. It is which column is being read. Otherwise we set the treasury, Maastricht, the cycle, an exemption and interest next to one another and are surprised that each figure wins a different contest.

A ceiling is not a direction. It is a choice of reference point. Below four hundred billion can be true — and a deepening at the same time.

2. Five different pictures of the budget balance

When “deficit” appears in a headline, it is easy to imagine a single invoice from the state. The result is produced in a chain. Each layer can raise the amount, lower it, or merely move it out of sight. Two sentences about the same year therefore need not be a quarrel. They can be two views of the same chain.

Five layers of one deficit

  1. State-budget cash deficit: the difference between revenue and expenditure of the state treasury, in koruna. Act No. 38/2026 Coll. set it at 310 billion koruna for 2026, with revenue of 2 117.8 billion and expenditure of 2 427.8 billion.[1] It measures the flow in one chapter of the state. It does not measure regions, municipalities or health insurers.
  2. General-government balance: ESA 2010, the notification to the European Commission, the ratio to GDP. It includes the wider public sector. It can differ from the cash deficit by accruals, capital injections and units outside the state budget.[12]
  3. Structural balance: the deficit adjusted for the cycle and one-off measures. On the 2026 proposal the National Budget Council reported the Ministry of Finance estimate of 2.2% of GDP and its own estimate of up to 2.5% of GDP. Both figures exceeded the legal limit then in force for that year. This is an estimate, not a cash balance.[4]
  4. Statutory spending framework: the ceiling under Act No. 23/2017 Coll. and its amendments. It says how much spending counts toward the limit. It does not say how much the state will actually spend if part of the spending drops out of the cap.[3][4]
  5. Financing need and debt service: how much must be borrowed and how much the interest costs. After the 2026 budget act took effect, the ministry put the total financing need at 737.8 billion koruna, about 8.2% of GDP. That is not the same thing as a deficit of 310 billion. It also includes repayments of old debt.[9]

The basic treasury sets an important part of the debate, but it is not the only actor. A government can formally keep the EU ratio to GDP and still raise nominal borrowing. It can show compliance with the framework because the content of the framework has changed. It can talk about investment while the item that builds nothing grows fastest: interest.

Public debate often compares figures from different accounting layers. Every comparison must therefore state the year, distinguish a proposal from an approved plan and from outturn, separate the cash balance from ESA, and explain the effect of the defence exemption.

The proposal raises the cash deficit from the planned 310 billion in 2026 to 389 billion in 2027. At the same time the ministry reports the sector balance, after the European defence clause, at −2.8% of GDP. Both figures can be true, because they measure different objects; neither by itself proves the “responsibility” of the budget.

Illustrative diagram: The same year can, in another layer, with another anchor and another exemption, produce another story.

3. The best deficit is the one whose error you can afford

The lowest number in a headline is not, by itself, the safest budget. The type of error, how easy it is to spot, and the cost of correcting it all have to be named. A badly rounded headline can be fixed quickly; a badly chosen reference base can hide a year-on-year deterioration. A debt-service trajectory and a legally anchored exemption are also harder to change than a single press sentence.

Risk does not rise only with the size of the deficit. The proposed 389 billion is a documented figure, but it does not by itself answer the question of the structural balance, of financing, or of future debt service.

Type of failure What it looks like How to test it What limits the harm
Anchor error The same amount read as consolidation and as expansion Ask: against which year, plan, or outturn? A mandatory year-on-year plan–plan and outturn–outturn table
Layer error Cash is passed off as Maastricht, and the reverse Separate the state budget (SR), ESA, the structural balance and the framework One sentence with every figure: what it measures, what it does not
Exemption error Compliance with a limit after an item is taken out Calculate the deficit with the exemption and without it A visible column “taken out of the framework”
Duration error A one-off outlay that becomes a trajectory Ask whether the exemption ends or is extended An end date for the clause and a plan after it

That also changes the reader’s question. We are not asking only which number is lower. We are asking which error in our reading we will catch. A voter can tolerate ten arguments about billions, because one direction is easy to see. A state accountant cannot tolerate a single hidden change in what counts toward the limit. An economist can accept a higher deficit if it is cyclical and temporary. An economist cannot accept it if it is structural and masked by an anchor from the covid year.

Beside the nominal ceiling it is necessary to watch whether the layers can be traced, how easily an outlay can be reversed, and what it will do to debt service. A clearly broken-down proposal may be more readable for scrutiny than a lower number without explanation.

4. What the deficit alone does not explain

A public balance is needed because it replaces part of impression with a repeatable series of numbers. The problem begins when one figure starts to stand for the whole of fiscal policy. The Ministry of Finance, the Czech Statistical Office and the National Budget Council measure different objects. None of them measures “responsibility”. That is a judgment, not an indicator.[4][12]

Every figure contains hidden decisions. Is it a plan, or outturn? The state budget, or general government? With EU projects, or without them? With defence spending above two percent of GDP, or without it? In which year’s prices, and in which GDP estimate? The answer to any of these questions can change the tone.

Five facts without which a deficit is only an advertising number

  • The exact unit: SR cash, ESA, the structural balance, or the framework.
  • The period and status: a proposal, an approved act, a cash outturn, a notification.
  • The population: central government, or the whole of general government.
  • What is taken out: EU funds, defence, one-off operations.
  • The known limit of the indicator: the cycle estimate, a GDP revision, NATO methodology versus ESA.

For buying political calm, therefore, a small internal table matters more than someone else’s overall ranking of the “worst deficit in history”. Eight real rows — with real chapters, Czech koruna, approved exemptions and actual debt service — can say more than a thousand comparisons with a year that will not be repeated. A public ceiling helps choose candidates for the headline. The decision has to be made by work with the layers.

That is not proof that ceilings are worthless. The point is that even an exact percentage can measure a defective condition exactly. Act No. 38/2026 Coll. sets 310 billion exactly. It does not say whether 310 billion is consolidation. It says what flow of the state treasury parliament approved for one year.[1]

5. Compare accounts, not headlines

The table ranks fiscal statements by the account they come from: the treasury, ESA, the structural balance, the spending framework or the debt. For each it shows the context and the swap that has to be caught in the reading.

SVI / ESA balance Notification, Maastricht, Eurostat Units outside the SR, accruals Comparison with three percent of GDP

The rows are not a verdict on the quality of a government. The same cabinet offers several figures in one workbook, and each of them can change by amendment within a few months. The map is meant to prevent a different error: that the public buys an excellent ceiling in an account that cannot see its question, cannot finish it safely, or locks it into an exemption that is later hard to leave.

How to read the diagram: The position or size of the elements does not express a score. The diagram separates the cash balance, the European indicators, the spending framework and debt service.

6. A cash deficit is not a sector balance

The domestic debate lives off the treasury. The state-budget act is intelligible: revenue, expenditure, deficit, debt cover. Act No. 38/2026 Coll. covers a deficit of 310 billion koruna and an increase in the balances of state financial-asset accounts by a change in the stock of state debt of 313.6 billion koruna. That is the flow of one accounting unit in one calendar year.[1]

The European debate lives off ESA 2010. The general-government balance includes the state budget, but also local government, health insurers and other units classified as government. Accrual accounting can move an outlay into a different year from the one in which it left the treasury. A capital injection can look like expenditure in one methodology and like a financial operation in another.[12][15]

When a government official argues that the deficit complies with EU rules, the official may be telling the truth about the SVI balance. That does nothing to refute the fact that the central government’s cash debt is rising in nominal terms. Nor the reverse: a deep cash deficit need not automatically mean a breach of the Maastricht ratio if GDP in the denominator is growing, or if part of the flow is not ESA expenditure.

What has to be tested

The first test concerns the unit. The same year in the treasury and in the notification is not the same experiment. The second concerns the adjustment: for 2025 the Ministry of Finance reported a cash deficit of 290.7 billion koruna and, after adjustment for EU projects and financial mechanisms, 249.9 billion. Both figures are true. They measure a different object. The first rose by 19.3 billion year on year against 2024. The second fell by 38.9 billion year on year. Whoever picks only one picks a story.[2]

The third test concerns plan versus outturn. The 2025 deficit exceeded the approved plan of 241 billion by 49.7 billion koruna. The figure 290.7 is therefore not evidence that “the plan is holding”. It is evidence that the treasury ended somewhere other than the act. That does not diminish the importance of the outturn. It reduces the right to claim that the approved balance is the same object as the balance achieved.[2][19]

For a reader who wants one sentence, cash may be decisive. For a reader who wants a European comparison, the sentence “we have 310 billion” is not enough. That reader needs ESA, the GDP estimate, the exemption, and whether the figure is a proposal or a notification.

7. History measures a different question from a plan

The path of the state-budget cash balance in recent years shows the band in which the Czech treasury is moving. It is not the pre-covid band of tens of billions. It is a band of hundreds.

2019 −28.5 bn CZK Outturn A pre-pandemic year. It does not measure today’s debt service or the defence commitment.
2020 −367.4 bn CZK Outturn The first pandemic year. A one-off shock, not a new “normal” written into law.
2021 −419.7 bn CZK Outturn The historical peak of the SR cash deficit. A poor anchor for a year without a lockdown.
2022 −360.4 bn CZK Outturn The energy crisis and the war. It measures a crisis flow, not a structural target.
2023 −288.5 bn CZK Outturn The start of the consolidation package. Still hundreds, not tens.

The table shows that deficits around 270 to 310 billion koruna have become the operating band of the Czech state, not an exceptional crisis episode. That is not proof that “everyone is stealing”. It is a description of the level at which the debate now moves. Whoever takes 2021 as the anchor gets a story of reassurance. Whoever takes 2019 gets a story of a break. Whoever takes the approved plan for 2026 gets the year-on-year direction. The data are identical. The tone depends on which row you put in the foreground.

The figure of 419.7 billion for 2021 measures the cash result of a crisis year. It does not say whether a proposal of 389 billion in 2027 is consolidation. The relevant plan comparison shows an increase against the approved 310 billion for 2026; the actual results of both years will be known only after they have ended.

8. An escape clause does not change the spending. It changes its visibility

Spending on a tank, a radar or ammunition is spending whether or not it counts toward the national limit. An escape clause does not change the physical delivery. It changes which part of the account appears in the rule.

At European level the defence escape clause allows a temporary deviation from the net-expenditure path. In the 2027 proposal the Ministry of Finance says it is not using the domestic backup escape clause, but in the European assessment it applies COFOG defence spending of CZK 70 billion: after that is taken into account it reports a balance of −2.8% of GDP, without it −3.5% of GDP.[7][8][22] The spending does not disappear from the cash budget; the clause changes the compliance assessment, while the effect on financing depends on the whole budget.

The National Budget Council said in 2025 that the European Commission’s illustrative projections, which assume linear use of the full flexibility through 2028, point to a deficit in 2028 up to 1.3% of GDP higher than if net expenditure had grown along the original path. The Council also stressed that the European clause does not by itself repeal the national fiscal-responsibility act. It is a Council recommendation on the EU framework. It is not an automatic breach of the debt brake.[5][4]

Three tests of an escape clause

The first is legal: which norm is being cited — Regulation 2024/1263, a Council recommendation, or Act No. 23/2017 Coll.? The second is about volume: how many billions are taken out, in which year and against which GDP forecast? The third is about time: does the flexibility end in 2028, or is the national exemption stretched further? A product that “meets the rule” a minute earlier need not be better if, a year later, no one can tell what still counted toward the rule.

In a security situation the clause may be strategically justified. It allows higher defence spending without the same consequence in the assessment of a chosen European rule; by itself it does not prove the cause of the whole cash deficit, nor the economic return on the spending.

9. Defence: the exception that became architecture

Since 2023 the Czech Republic has had Act No. 177/2023 Coll., which requires the government, in the draft state budget, to set defence spending at least at 2% of nominal GDP according to the forecast in the budget strategy. That is not a promise from a press conference. It is a statutory share. It is calculated from the forecast, not from later revised GDP. Final recognition in NATO methodology arrives with a lag and may differ from the sum of the Ministry of Defence chapter.[13]

The executive summary of the 2026 state budget puts total defence spending in all chapters at 184.6 billion koruna. On the January macroeconomic forecast that was 2.06% of GDP, on the April forecast 2.1% of GDP. Year on year these outlays in all chapters rise by 23.9 billion. The figure of 184.6 billion is therefore not “the MoD chapter”. It is the sum of the items that count toward defence. The defence chapter itself is lower. Whoever swaps them manufactures an argument about tens of billions that is an argument about definitions.[11]

At national level the law allows certain defence spending to be treated outside the ordinary path of the spending frameworks.[3][5] The exact scope and duration have to be read in the current wording of the act and in the proposal tables; this article, without a further primary source, does not set a horizon into the thirties. The spending remains spending; what changes is its treatment in the rule.

That is an important distinction. Defence investment can be legitimate and strategically necessary. At the same time it can worsen the cash deficit and debt service. Both sentences can be true. Whoever infers from the first that the second is slander is mixing a security aim with a fiscal layer. Whoever infers from the second that the first is unnecessary is mixing an account with a threat.

Before reading the defence chapter, therefore, “how much does it cost” is not enough. It is necessary to know whether the figure is 2% or a band above it, whether it is calculated from the January or the April GDP forecast, whether NATO will recognise the same items, and whether the amount fits the framework only because the framework dropped the amount.

10. A law adapted to the budget. Not the other way around

On the draft state budget for 2026 the National Budget Council said that the binding deficit limit under the fiscal-responsibility rules act is 246 billion koruna and that the proposal with a deficit of 310 billion exceeds that ceiling by 64 billion. According to the Council this was the first case, for as long as the act has been in force, in which the government proposal itself was ex ante in explicit conflict with the maximum statutory limits. The Ministry of Finance rejected the mismatch by pointing to the European escape clause and the precedence of EU law in application. That is a dispute about the hierarchy of norms. It is not a dispute about whether 310 is larger than 246.[4]

In its opinion on public finances in 2025 the Council also warned that the present setting of tax and spending policies does not secure the consolidation path after 2026, and that in an unchanged-policy scenario the structural deficit will already in 2027 be higher than the act assumes — even after additional defence spending with the exemption is subtracted. That is one institution’s projection, not the cash result for 2027. It does, however, say something that a ceiling of “under 400” does not: the direction after the election year is not automatically a continuation of consolidation.[5]

By shifting spending frameworks and creating exemptions, the political representation makes legal room so that it need not proceed to further cuts or tax changes. That is not necessarily a breach of the law in the criminal sense. The point is that the law can adapt to the government’s budgetary needs, not only the budget to the law. That is legitimate work for parliament. It is also why the sentence “we kept the rules” requires a question: which rules, in which wording, after which amendment.

As of 31 August 2026 there is an official Ministry of Finance proposal with a deficit of CZK 389 billion.[22] It is not an independent audit, an approved act or an accounting fact. On compliance with national rules and on the consolidation direction it is therefore necessary to read, beside the proposal, the current NRR opinions and the later changes in debate.

11. How to check a claim about the deficit

After the proposal was published, 389 billion is no longer a mere political corridor. The reader has to check that it is a proposal submitted to the government, not an act or an outturn, and whether the claim uses the cash deficit, the sector balance or a structural indicator.

One press conference can join three true figures into a false conclusion: the outturn of 419.7 billion in 2021, the approved plan of 310 billion in 2026 and the proposal of 389 billion in 2027. The synthesis “we are consolidating because we are under four hundred” does not stand as a year-on-year plan comparison; against 2026 the proposal deepens by 79 billion.

A four-step check of a budget statement

  1. Existence: the figure is in an act, in the cash outturn, or only in a sentence.
  2. Authority: the source is primary for that layer — the Ministry of Finance, the Czech Statistical Office, the NRR, Eurostat.
  3. Support: a specific passage actually supports the specific sentence about direction.
  4. Scope: the year, the unit, the adjustment, the exemption and whether it is a plan all fit.

The most reliable working method therefore separates searching from writing. First a table of claims, primary sources and limits is built. Only then the headline. For every figure, the population, the period, the indicator measured and the uncertainty are recorded. If the source cannot be opened or the passage cannot be found, a claim is not promoted to a fact merely because it is worded calmly.

A budget statement is good when it shortens the path to a check, not when it replaces the check. For a low-risk question a quick sentence about the ceiling may suffice. For an act that will bind borrowing for the next year, the last step has to be taken by a person who understands the layer.

12. Debt service is not a one-off item

Chapter 396 – State Debt, under Act No. 218/2000 Coll., gathers the revenue and expenditure tied to managing the liquidity of the state treasury and managing the state debt. The administrator is the Ministry of Finance. It is mostly interest on government bonds and loans taken, not an “accounting operation” that could be postponed without a price.[10][14]

The executive summary of the 2026 budget puts spending on servicing the state debt at 110.0 billion koruna, 10.0 billion more year on year. The figure measures budgeted interest and related spending, not the principal or the future rate on refinancing. Debt service is a contractually and market-wise hard-to-postpone flow, but not a category that can, without qualification, be labelled a “mandatory expenditure” in the same sense as statutory benefits.[11]

By the end of 2025, according to the finance minister’s public figure, state debt had risen to 3.678 trillion koruna. That is a stock, not a deficit. The 2025 deficit of 290.7 billion is a flow that feeds into the stock, but is not identical with it. Whoever adds them into one sentence about a “four trillion deficit” is mixing a stock and a flow.[19][2]

The illustration is simple. Each further year with a deficit around 300 billion koruna adds principal that will have to be rolled over later. Even if the next deficit returned to zero, the interest on the principal already there would not disappear. A ceiling of “under 400” can therefore be true in 2027 and still leave in chapter 396 a bill that will be visible in 2030. That is not a forecast of rates. It is the mechanics of a bond.

13. The price of the raw material is not the price of the result

The market uses several fiscal languages at once. Politics pays with a visible ceiling. The treasury pays with a cash flow. The market pays with the bond yield. The European Commission pays with a net-expenditure path. A direct comparison of price lists without knowing the layer therefore misleads.

A cheap deficit in a headline can be economically excellent if it is cyclical, temporary and covered by growth. The same figure can be expensive if it repeats, if defence has dropped out of the framework, and if chapter 396 is growing by ten billion a year. An expensive deficit in a covid year can be useless as an anchor for 2027. It can still be useful as a warning of how far the treasury can go.

The real price of an adopted budget

Cash deficit + spending taken out + interest + refinancing + the price of postponing consolidation + dependence on the exemption.

The result can be judged only once the treasury deficit, the ESA balance and debt service have been explained against one another. The same number cannot be demanded: each metric has a different definition.

The cost also includes volatility. If the same ceiling is defended only thanks to the anchor year 2021, the average price of truthfulness is not the price of one press briefing. If the system slows at a peak of maturities, or exhausts the market’s willingness, the cost is the yield. If the exemptions are tightly bound to one reading of EU law, the cost is also a future dispute with the NRR.

It is therefore useful to measure the price of the direction taken, not the price of the headline. For the 389 billion proposal that means describing the year-on-year change, the defence clause, the financing and the uncertainty before the act is passed.

14. What to read in the proposal before a verdict is reached

The budget workbook should be read according to the specific question and the accounting layer. The table shows where to compare plan with outturn and the cash balance with ESA methodology, without mixing years or units.

Need First place in the workbook The decisive test Unacceptable shortcut
Year-on-year direction SR deficit 2027 versus the 2026 act The same unit, plan versus plan The 2021 anchor
EU compliance SVI balance and the net-expenditure path ESA, GDP, NEC “We are under 3%, done”
Compliance with national law The framework under 23/2017 Coll. The volume of defence taken out Citing the framework after an amendment as if it were the original
The price of the past Chapter 396 Year-on-year growth of interest A deficit without interest
Investment versus operations Capital versus current spending What is one-off Calling every outlay an investment
Defence The sum of all chapters, not only the MoD The GDP forecast, NATO methodology Passing 184.6 bn off as the MoD chapter

For Czech use a separate anchor test is required. An English commentary on the Stability Pact will not measure whether the sentence “under 400 billion” in Czech means a ceiling or a promise. The test set should include the 2026 plan, the 2025 outturn, the covid record, budgeted debt service and the CZK 70 billion of COFOG defence spending stated in the 2027 proposal. It should also include a sentence in which the right answer is “the source is not enough”.

Some readers finally need more figures. One for the treasury, a second for ESA, a third for interest and a fourth for defence taken out. That is not a failure of clarity. It is the same decision as using one account for a household and another for a firm. The condition is that the split is intelligible, that the figures do not cross unchecked, and that people know where final authority arises — in the Chamber, in a notification, or in a press sentence.

After the proposal is published and before the act is passed

  • Separate the Ministry of Finance proposal from an act approved by the government and parliament.
  • Write down the year-on-year difference against the 310 billion of 2026.
  • Establish the volume of defence in all chapters and the volume above 2% of GDP.
  • Open chapter 396 and its year-on-year change.
  • Check whether the structural estimate cites the Ministry of Finance or the NRR.

15. From a ministry proposal to an approved act

A position on the proposal can be chosen without a six-month seminar and without impulsive agreement with the headline. A short, disciplined reading pilot is enough. Its aim is not to prove that the government can do something impressive. Its aim is to find situations in which a sentence about the ceiling fails, and to see whether we can catch them.

A fortnight’s check of the proposal

  1. Days 1–2: Write down the layers: cash, ESA, the structural balance, the framework, interest.
  2. Days 3–4: Prepare the same anchors — 2019, 2021, 2025, 2026 — and forbid mixing plan with outturn.
  3. Days 5–7: Repeat every important statement three times: with the ceiling, with the direction, with the exemption.
  4. Days 8–10: Check the clauses, the volume of spending taken out, the end date and chapter 396.
  5. Days 11–12: Calculate the price of the direction taken, including interest, not only the price of the headline.
  6. Day 13: Stress tests: the covid anchor, the EU adjustment, defence above 2% of GDP, the absence of an act.
  7. Day 14: Document the limits and set the next review after the government decision and after submission to the Chamber.

If possible the assessor should not begin with the sentence they want to confirm. On cash they follow the flow. On ESA, the support in the notification. On the framework, the content of the limit. On defence, the definition of the chapters. On interest, the year-on-year change. Each failure is classified: anchor, layer, exemption, duration, or a missing source.

The conditions have to be comparable. The same year, the same unit, the same status of the proposal. If one statement may use 2021 and another 2026, we are testing rhetoric, which may be entirely proper — but we have to name it as such. If we want to test consolidation, we have to unify the anchor on plan–plan.

The pilot should also have elimination conditions. For example: a single swap of plan and outturn in the decisive sentence, the use of the covid record as proof of discipline, or an inability to trace how much defence dropped out of the framework. A high average score for intelligibility must not override an error whose impact the reader cannot accept.

The last output is not merely agreement with the ceiling. It is an operating rule: what the figure is used for, what it is not, which layer may enter the headline, what must stay in the table, and when the decision will be opened again. Acts, forecasts and clauses change. Without a revision date even a good ceiling gradually turns into a state superstition.

16. Maastricht under three percent is not consolidation

The deficit-to-GDP ratio is a useful map. The Maastricht threshold of 3% of GDP is a procedural threshold, not a thermometer of virtue. It can be met by growth in the denominator, a one-off receipt, a shift of spending outside the sector, or an actual cut in the flow. Whoever announces “we are meeting Maastricht” has announced compliance with one threshold. They have not announced that the cash deficit has fallen year on year. They have not announced that debt service is falling. They have not announced that the structural balance is heading toward one percent of GDP, as the national act speaks of it.[8][3][6]

In 2025 the NRR recalled the long-term importance of the structural balance and doubted that, without further measures, the national fiscal rules would be met in 2027.[6][5] That opinion predates the Ministry of Finance proposal of 31 August 2026 and is not an assessment of it. It has to be updated against the new proposal, which is not yet an act or an outturn.

OECD and Eurostat international comparisons help only where the definition fits. The Czech ESA deficit cannot, without reservation, be set beside a neighbouring country’s cash deficit. Even the debt-to-GDP ratio does not measure interest-rate risk at a short maturity. When the definition fits badly, it is more honest to say so than to manufacture a ranking.

Consolidation is the direction after adjustment for the cycle, one-off operations and exemptions. A ceiling is the upper bound of one year. They can meet. They need not. A sentence that swaps them is not a lie about the amount. It is a mistake about the question.

17. The price of the exemption in later budgets

The first generation of budget debates sold an absolute figure in billions. The next generation sold a ratio to GDP. Today’s workbooks sell something larger: a place in which part of the spending becomes invisible to the rule that was meant to constrain it.

Governments therefore compete not only for the lowest deficit. They compete for the reference base. For which year is the anchor, which layer enters the headline, which clause is activated and which interest is postponed into a chapter that no one applauds. The more comfortable the ceiling that arises, the higher the price of leaving the exemption can be. Frameworks are hard to put back, consolidation automatics rely on the original act, and people stop distinguishing their own rule from the amendment in which they rewrote it.

That does not mean a defence exemption is a bad thing. Precisely that exemption can create the greatest benefit in a security situation. It means it should be bought consciously. The public needs a portable table of layers, a visible column of spending taken out, a way to compare plan with plan, and an emergency reading procedure without a headline.

The best deficit is therefore not the one that is never deep. After a pandemic, a war and an act on two percent of GDP for defence, we do not have such a year. Nor is it the deficit with the lowest number, the longest ceiling or the largest number of references to Maastricht. It is the deficit whose layers match the real question and whose errors are visible, reversible and economically bearable.

At the moment 389 billion is published, we are reading a ministry proposal. Only government and parliamentary debate can turn it into an approved budget; later outturn will show the fact. At the same time the framework, the defence clause and the refinancing of the debt have to be watched.

What decides is against which year the proposal deepens, which spending changes the fiscal assessment, and how that feeds through into financing and interest.
